# Recovery Roadmap: What to Expect Each Month

*"How long will this last?" — the first question everyone asks. The answer: 2 to 6 months with daily effort. But those months aren't structured the way you think. Recovery isn't a straight line. It's a spiral. And if you know what's around the corner — you won't panic at every setback.*

---

## Month 1: Chaos and First Anchors

**What's happening:** You just started. Read the first few chapters. You're doing Jacobson and vestibular exercises. Still wobbling just as much. Sometimes — even more (because you've started paying attention). You feel like nothing is working.

**What's actually changing:** You've stopped running from doctor to doctor. You understand that the hardware is fine. You know the word "PPPD" and you know it won't kill you. That's already a lot — you've exited the "unknown disease" phase.

**Common traps:**
- "I've been doing the exercises for 3 days and I'm not better" — the exercises start working in 2–4 weeks. This isn't paracetamol.
- "After exercises the wobbling is worse!" — that's normal. The vestibular system is calibrating. Increased symptoms after training = proper workload.
- The urge to read the whole book in one night — don't. One chapter a day. Absorb it, do the homework, then move on.

**DHI (Dizziness Handicap Index):** 50–70 points. Good days: 1–2 per week.

---

## Month 2: First Glimpses

**What's happening:** "Windows" appear — hours or even whole days when the unsteadiness retreats. Then it comes back. You feel like you're on a roller coaster: morning is good → evening is bad → tomorrow is good again. It drives you crazy.

**What's actually changing:** The "windows" are proof of neuroplasticity. Your brain is building new connections. They're still fragile — like a fresh trail in the snow. One stress — and you get swept back onto the old highway. But the trail already exists.

**Common traps:**
- "Yesterday was good, today is bad again — that means a relapse!" — this isn't a relapse. This is normal wave-like progression. A real relapse is a return to Month 1 levels.
- Comparing yourself to others: "Pete recovered in a month, and I haven't" — everyone has their own pace. Compare yourself to yourself a month ago.

**DHI:** 35–55 points. Good days: 3–4 per week.

---

## Month 3: The First Serious Setback

**What's happening:** You've gotten used to the "windows." You've started going outside. Maybe even went back to work. And then — bam. The unsteadiness comes back like it's the first week. Panic: "It was all for nothing! I'll never get better!"

**What's actually happening:** This is **normal.** The classic Month 3 setback — documented in scientific literature. The trigger is usually: stress, sleep deprivation, illness, an emotional event. The brain temporarily switched back to the old highway because the new trail isn't beaten down yet.

**What to do:**
1. Don't panic. Re-read the chapter "Anatomy of a Setback."
2. Return to the base protocol: Jacobson + vestibular exercises + walking. Every day.
3. Time it: the setback lasts 3–7 days. Not 3 months. Not "forever." Days.

**DHI:** Spikes to 45–60, then drops. Good days: 2–3 per week (temporarily).

---

## Month 4–5: The New Normal

**What's happening:** The unsteadiness is still there, but you stop thinking about it. Not through willpower — but because your brain has adapted. You go to the store, to work, to the gym. Sometimes you catch a slight swaying — and you don't pay attention. Like road noise outside the window: it's there, but you don't hear it.

**What's actually changing:** The CAS trap is weakening. You stop body-scanning every 10 minutes. Exposure has paid off — the brain has convinced itself that the store won't kill you. The neural fear pathway is thinning out.

**Common traps:**
- "I'm 80% better, but 20% remains — maybe this isn't PPPD anymore, maybe it's something else?" — this is a classic trap. That 20% is the tail. It fades. Don't start a new round of tests.
- Dropping the practice: "I'm better now, I can stop doing the exercises." No. At least 2–3 more months.

**DHI:** 15–30 points. Good days: 5–6 per week.

---

## Month 6+: The Exit

**What's happening:** You forget the last time you thought about PPPD. There's no unsteadiness — or it's so background that you only notice it when someone asks. You're living. Just living.

**What actually happened:** Neuroplasticity worked. The new trail became a highway. The old fear highway — an overgrown path. The vestibular system is calibrated. The muscle armor has relaxed. The CAS trap has closed.

**What's next:**
- Keep exercising — it's your insurance against relapse
- If unsteadiness returns six months later during intense stress — don't panic. This isn't "PPPD coming back." It's a normal body reaction to stress. Run the protocol for 3–5 days — and you're done.
- Help someone who's currently at Month 1. Seriously. It's the best insurance: you cement your position as "healthy," not "formerly sick."

---

## Honest Statistics

| Metric | Month 1 | Month 2 | Month 3 | Month 4–5 | Month 6+ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| DHI (points) | 50–70 | 35–55 | 45–60* | 15–30 | 0–10 |
| Good days/week | 1–2 | 3–4 | 2–3* | 5–6 | 7 |
| Exposure | Entrance hall | Store | — * | Mall, subway | Everything |
| Main enemy | Fear | Impatience | Setback panic | Dropped practices | Tail hypochondria |

*\* Month 3 — setback. This is normal. It passes.*
