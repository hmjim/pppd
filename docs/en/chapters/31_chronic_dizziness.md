# Constant Dizziness: Causes, When It's PPPD, and What to Do

*Have you been swaying and rocking for weeks or months while doctors shrug at your clean test results? Chances are it's PPPD—and it is treatable.*

---

## Why Am I Constantly Dizzy?

If your dizziness has lasted **longer than 3 months**, it isn't "just nerves" and it isn't lingering fallout from a cold. It is a recognized chronic condition with a specific clinical name: **PPPD**—Persistent Postural-Perceptual Dizziness (ICD-11: AB32.0).

PPPD is the second most common diagnosis made by neuro-otologists worldwide, diagnosed in **15–20% of all patients** seen in specialized vestibular clinics. Yet most sufferers spend years bouncing from doctor to doctor without ever getting an accurate diagnosis.

## Types of Dizziness: PPPD vs. Other Vestibular Conditions

Not all dizziness is PPPD. Here is how to tell them apart:

### PPPD (Persistent Postural-Perceptual Dizziness)
- A sensation of **rocking, swaying, bobbing, or unsteadiness**—not true room-spinning vertigo
- Lasts **hours, days, or weeks** continuously
- Worse when standing upright or walking; eases up when lying down
- Flares up in visually busy spaces (supermarkets, subways, crowds)
- Every lab test and brain MRI comes back **completely normal**
- Frequently accompanied by anxiety

### BPPV (Benign Paroxysmal Positional Vertigo)
- **Intense rotational spinning** lasting 10–60 seconds
- Triggered by rolling over or turning your head in bed
- Resolved in 1–2 sessions with the Epley maneuver

### Vestibular Migraine
- Episodes of dizziness or vertigo + **headache**
- Lasts minutes to hours, then clears between episodes
- Treated with targeted migraine prevention

### Ménière's Disease
- Attacks of rotational spinning + **roaring tinnitus (ear ringing)** + hearing loss
- Lasts 20 minutes to several hours
- Leaves characteristic low-frequency changes on an audiogram

## Constant Unsteadiness When Walking: Why Your Legs Feel Like Jelly

The single most common complaint in PPPD is **"I feel unsteady whenever I walk."** That happens because your brain has locked into **Visual Dependence**—relying overwhelmingly on your eyes to manage balance while down-weighting your inner-ear vestibular system and proprioception.

Every time you walk, the visual scene streams continuously across your retina. An over-vigilant brain misinterprets that normal optical motion as physical instability—giving you "jelly legs" (that spongy, cotton-wool sensation underfoot), a floating floor, and boat-deck rocking.

For a deeper look at how this works, see [What Is PPPD: Symptoms and Causes](01_what_is_pppg.html).

## Brain Fog Alongside Dizziness

If your unsteadiness comes with **brain fog**—mental heaviness, cotton-headedness, and an inability to concentrate—that is a textbook companion symptom of PPPD.

Why it happens:
- **CPU overload in the brain** — your brain is burning nearly all its processing power trying to micromanage balance on manual control
- **Chronic anxiety** — persistently elevated cortisol blunts memory and executive focus
- **Derealization** — the brain's protective circuit breaker under relentless stress

Brain fog clears automatically as your PPPD resolves. You do not need to treat it separately.

## What to Do When Dizziness Won't Stop

### Step 1: Rule Out Organic Causes
Complete the baseline medical workup: brain MRI, neck vessel Doppler ultrasound, a neuro-otologist exam, and audiometry. You will find the full checklist in [What Medical Tests to Get](02_medical_checkup.html).

### Step 2: Accept the PPPD Diagnosis
If your test results are clean, this is PPPD. Not a "mystery illness." Not "hopeless psychosomatics you just have to live with." It is a concrete diagnosis with a concrete, evidence-based recovery protocol.

### Step 3: Start Multimodal Rehab
The five pillars that work for PPPD:
1. Vestibular rehabilitation ([exercises](06_vestibular.html))
2. Cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT)
3. Graded exposure to triggering situations
4. Regular physical exercise
5. SSRIs (when prescribed by a physician)

Full guide: [PPPD Treatment: The Complete Guide](30_treatment_overview.html).

### Step 4: Release Chronic Muscle Tension
PPPD is virtually always accompanied by chronic protective muscle bracing. Progressive muscle relaxation (Jacobson's technique) is a proven tool for shutting it down: [Step-by-Step Instructions](05_relaxation.html).

## Frequently Asked Questions

**"I'm 25 / 30 / 40 years old. Can you really get PPPD at my age?"**
Yes. PPPD most commonly begins between ages 20 and 50, often sparked by severe stress, a panic attack, or a resolved bout of BPPV.

**"My dizziness started from stress. Is this PPPD?"**
If dizziness persists for more than 3 months after a stressful event, that meets one of the core criteria for PPPD. Stress was the trigger—not ongoing damage.

**"Can PPPD go away on its own?"**
In rare cases, yes—if stress levels drop naturally and you don't slip into avoidance. Far more often, without targeted retraining, PPPD locks into a chronic loop.

**"Are 'cervical osteochondrosis' or 'VSD' causing my dizziness?"**
No. "Cervical osteochondrosis" (routine age-related neck wear misdiagnosed as a cause of dizziness) appears on scans in 90% of adults over 30 and does not cause chronic dizziness. And "VSD" (a post-Soviet catch-all label for autonomic dysfunction) is an outdated wastebasket diagnosis that does not exist in international medical classifications.
