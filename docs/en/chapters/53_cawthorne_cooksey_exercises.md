# Cawthorne-Cooksey Vestibular Exercises for PPPD: Timeline, Stages & Protocols

Vestibular Rehabilitation Therapy (VRT) constitutes the structural core of PPPD recovery. The classic **Cawthorne-Cooksey Exercises** protocol was specifically developed to stimulate central vestibular compensation and retrain cerebellar balance computation.

Below is the clinical stage breakdown, correct dosing rules, and expected adaptation timelines.

---

## 1. The Core Principle: Why Exercises Must Trigger Mild Symptoms

The fundamental law of neuroplastic balance adaptation: **The brain only recalibrates when it detects sensory computation error.**

Performing drills too slowly or passively stimulates zero adaptation. Conversely, over-exercising causes sensory exhaustion and symptom setbacks.
The optimal training threshold: **provoke mild, tolerable dizziness rated at 3–4 out of 10**, which completely subsides within 2–3 minutes after stopping the exercise.

---

## 2. Progressive Stages of Cawthorne-Cooksey Training:

### Stage 1: Seated Drills (Weeks 1–2)
1. **Gaze Motion:** Moving eyes up-down and side-to-side, progressing from slow to rapid tracking (20 repetitions).
2. **Near-Point Fixation:** Tracking a finger moving toward the nose from 30 cm to 10 cm and away.
3. **Head Motion:** Tilting head up-down and turning side-to-side with eyes open, then with eyes closed.

### Stage 2: Standing Drills (Weeks 2–4)
1. Head rotations and pitch tilts performed while standing upright.
2. Passing or tossing a small rubber ball from hand to hand at eye level.
3. Transitioning smoothly from sitting to standing with eyes open, then eyes closed.

### Stage 3: Dynamic Locomotion Drills (Weeks 4–8)
1. Walking across the room while simultaneously turning head left-right on each alternating step.
2. Figure-8 walking patterns around two chairs.
3. Romberg stance on compliant foam or balance pads.

---

## 3. Recovery Timeline: What to Expect

* **Weeks 1–2:** Transient symptom flare-ups (initial sensory recalibration).
* **Weeks 4–6:** Marked reduction in visual supermarket sensitivity and head fog.
* **Months 3–6:** Complete automation of gaze stability and full return of steady gait.
