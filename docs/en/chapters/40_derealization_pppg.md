# Derealization in PPPD — Why the World Feels Unreal

*On top of the unsteadiness, the world has started feeling "fake." Like it's behind glass. Like a dream. Like a movie you're watching but not part of. Sound familiar? That's derealization. And it's not madness. It's a circuit breaker.*

## What Derealization Is

**Derealization** is the feeling that the world around you isn't real:
- Everything is "behind glass" or "inside an aquarium"
- The world looks flat, "cardboard," like a stage set
- Colors are washed out — or on the contrary, painfully vivid
- Sounds are muffled — or on the contrary, feel like they're drilling into your skull
- A "fog" or "haze" in front of your eyes
- Familiar places feel unfamiliar — you're in your own apartment, but it feels like someone else's

**Depersonalization** is similar, but it's about you:
- "My hands don't feel like mine"
- "I'm watching myself from the outside"
- "My emotions are dulled — I don't feel joy, I don't feel sadness"
- "I'm like a robot" — mechanically going through the motions, but not really "here"

This is absolutely terrifying. Many people think they're losing their mind. That it's schizophrenia. That their brain is "broken for good."

No. This is not psychosis. You are **not** losing your mind.

## Why Derealization Happens in PPPD

Imagine a circuit breaker in your electrical panel. When the load on the wiring spikes too high — the breaker trips. The lights go out. But the house is fine. The wiring didn't burn. The breaker saved it.

Derealization is that same circuit breaker. Except it's a neurological one.

In PPPD, anxiety surges so high that the brain "can't handle it." Too many incoming signals: unsteadiness, fear, hypervigilance, muscle spasms, adrenaline. The CPU is overloaded. And the brain does exactly what any overloaded computer would do — **it deprioritizes non-essential processes**.

It "dims" the brightness of perception. Colors fade. Sounds muffle. Emotions flatten. You feel like you're "behind glass." It's uncomfortable, but it's **protection from overload**. The brain is saving you from burnout.

### The Mechanism

1. **Chronic stress** → constant release of cortisol and adrenaline
2. **Nervous system overload** → the brain can't keep up with the flood of signals
3. **"Power-saving mode" kicks in** → perception brightness drops = derealization
4. **Fear of derealization** → "I'm going crazy!" → more anxiety → even stronger derealization

The same [adrenaline loop](09_adrenaline_loop.html), just expressed not through unsteadiness, but through perception.

## "But What If It's Schizophrenia?"

No. In schizophrenia, a person **doesn't realize** that their perception is distorted. They believe their hallucinations are real. They don't ask themselves "am I going crazy?"

You, on the other hand, understand that "something is off." You have **insight** into your condition. That's the key difference. If you can ask "am I losing my mind?" — you're not losing your mind. Psychosis doesn't ask questions.

## Connection to Other PPPD Symptoms

Derealization in PPPD almost always comes bundled with:
- [Brain fog](33_brain_fog.html) — heaviness, "cotton wool," sluggishness. Derealization + fog = "I feel like I'm wrapped in cotton, the world is wrapped in mist"
- [Visual dependence](08_visual_dependence.html) — visual overload triggers derealization too
- [Muscle armor](04_muscle_armor.html) — a locked-up neck → impaired venous outflow → worse "fog" and unreality
- [Panic attacks](68_panic_attacks.html) — derealization often accompanies the peak of panic

## What Does NOT Help

- ❌ **Googling "derealization schizophrenia"** — no, in PPPD this is neurotic derealization, not psychotic. Google doesn't know that and will serve you horror stories.
- ❌ **Fighting the feeling** — the harder you try to "bring reality back," the stronger the anxiety → the stronger the derealization. Resistance amplifies the symptom.
- ❌ **Hiding at home in a dark room** — isolation doesn't heal. It preserves the condition. The brain needs stimulation to rewire itself.
- ❌ **Nootropics** — mexidol, actovegin, cerebrolysin have zero evidence base for neurotic derealization. None whatsoever.

## What Does Help

1. **Accept it.** Derealization is not dangerous. It's a circuit breaker. When anxiety drops — the breaker will reset, and the world will get its colors back. You don't need to fix this separately.

2. **Lower the anxiety.** [Metacognitive therapy](15_metacognition.html), [relaxation](05_relaxation.html), working with [cognitive distortions](16_cognitive_distortions.html). Anxiety = fuel for derealization. Cut the fuel — and the fire goes out.

3. **Physical activity.** [Exercise](13_sport.html) lowers cortisol and brings back your **sense of body**. After a good workout, derealization weakens — because the brain shifts its focus to physical signals.

4. **Exposure.** [Gradual immersion](12_exposure.html) into frightening situations. The brain becomes convinced there's no danger → scales back "power-saving mode" → perception returns.

5. **Fix your sleep.** [Sleep and PPPD](26_sleep.html) are tightly linked. Sleep deprivation amplifies derealization dramatically.

## Derealization Goes Away

It will pass. This is one of the scariest, yet most reversible symptoms of PPPD. For most people, derealization fades first — even before the unsteadiness fully resolves.
