# PPPD and Daily Life: Work, Disability, Driving, and Quality of Life

Chronic unsteadiness, cognitive brain fog, and sensory exhaustion from PPPD profoundly impact vocational performance and everyday activities. Patients frequently face complex occupational, legal, and lifestyle dilemmas.

---

## 1. PPPD in the Workplace: Overcoming Visual Fatigue

Working in modern office environments, retail, or tech roles introduces severe sensory triggers:
* **Visual Screen Load:** Scrolling through complex spreadsheets, multi-monitor setups, and fluorescent light flicker;
* **Brain Fog:** Sluggish information processing, working memory fatigue, and difficulty concentrating;
* **Static Postural Strain:** Prolonged seated postures aggravate suboccipital cervical spasms.

*Clinical Recommendation:* Implement 5-minute sensory breaks every hour (gazing out the window at distant infinity, 4-7-8 breathing, shoulder somatic drops).

---

## 2. Sick Leave and Medical Certification

Physicians can issue medical leave for acute PPPD exacerbations. Under **ICD-11, PPPD is officially recognized with diagnostic code AB32.0**. Under ICD-10, clinicians frequently utilize equivalent categorizations such as *H81.8 (Other vestibular dysfunction)*, *G43 (Vestibular migraine)*, or *F45.3 (Somatoform autonomic dysfunction)*.

---

## 3. Operating Motor Vehicles with PPPD

PPPD does not cause sudden loss of consciousness, blackouts, or seizures. However, during periods of severe visual dependency and disequilibrium (heavy traffic, highway rain driving, nighttime glare), long-distance driving should be temporarily moderated until sensory recalibration is achieved through VRT.

---

## 4. Military Service & Disability Evaluation

Military medical boards evaluate vestibular and functional chronic disorders under specific functional disability criteria. Comprehensive clinical documentation including audiometry, vHIT, posturography, and psychometric profiles is required to confirm diagnostic status.
