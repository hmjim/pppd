# Medications for PPPD: What Works and What's a Waste of Money

*Phenibut, Mexidol, Phenazepam, Teraligen, "vascular drips"... Familiar list? Let's sort out which pills actually help with PPPD, which are placebo, and which are downright dangerous.*

## Nootropics and "Vascular" Drugs: Mexidol, Cavinton, Cerebrolysin, Eltacin

**Effectiveness for PPPD: zero.**

I know this is hard to hear. Especially if you've already sat through a couple of Cerebrolysin IV courses, 10 vials at a time. Or popped three boxes of Mexidol. Or shelled out serious money for IV drips "to improve cerebral blood flow."

But here's the fact: PPPD is **not a brain circulation disorder**. It's not a "pinched artery in the neck." Not "dyscirculatory encephalopathy." Not a "vascular spasm." All those diagnoses you were given — garbage.

PPPD is a software bug. The software is glitching, not the hardware. You wouldn't replace the graphics card when you have a virus on your computer, right? Well, Mexidol is basically trying to "replace the graphics card." Pointless.

Nootropics have no evidence base for PPPD. There isn't a single randomized clinical trial confirming their effectiveness for functional dizziness. Not one. Period.

If you "felt a bit better" after Mexidol — that's the placebo effect. A powerful thing, by the way. But you don't need to pay a fortune for a placebo.

## Tranquilizers: Phenazepam, Clonazepam, Hydroxyzine

**Dangerous territory. Read carefully.**

Benzodiazepines (Phenazepam, Clonazepam) are like pressing "Mute" on your speakers. One press — silence. Anxiety gone, unsteadiness reduced, the world becomes soft and fuzzy. Bliss.

But there are three problems:

**First** — they are **vestibular suppressants**. They suppress the vestibular nuclei in the brain. This means that while you're taking them, your brain **cannot relearn**. [Neuroplasticity](14_neuroplasticity.html) is blocked. You're stuffing antifreeze into a broken window — you don't feel the cold, but the hole is still there.

**Second** — tolerance. After 2–4 weeks of regular use, your brain adapts, and you need a higher dose for the same effect. Classic tolerance. And then comes withdrawal: rebound anxiety, insomnia, unsteadiness **far worse** than before you started.

**Third** — a false sense of control. You're not learning to cope with anxiety. You're learning to swallow a pill. And when the pills run out — you're left face-to-face with panic that's only gotten stronger in the meantime.

Phenazepam is acceptable **only** as emergency relief during a severe panic attack — a single dose, not a course. Like a fire extinguisher — for emergencies, not for daily heating.

## SSRIs and SNRIs: The Gold Standard

This is evidence-based medicine. International guidelines (Bárány Society, Mayo Clinic, Cochrane reviews) identify antidepressants from the **SSRI** group (Sertraline, Escitalopram, Paroxetine) and **SNRI** group (Venlafaxine, Duloxetine) as first-line treatments for PPPD.

### How They Work for Dizziness

These aren't "happy pills." For PPPD, they do three specific things:

**1.** They increase serotonin levels in the **vestibular nuclei of the brainstem**. Serotonin 5-HT1A receptors directly inhibit pathological hyperexcitability of vestibular neurons. In plain terms: the brain stops "screaming" at every micro-movement.

**2.** They break the [adrenaline loop](09_adrenaline_loop.html). SSRIs reduce baseline anxiety — and the cycle of "fear → adrenaline → unsteadiness → more fear" collapses.

**3.** They stimulate the production of **BDNF** (brain-derived neurotrophic factor) — a kind of "fertilizer" for new neural connections. SSRIs accelerate [neuroplasticity](14_neuroplasticity.html). The brain learns faster.

### Important Details

- **They start working after 3–6 weeks**. Not after 3 days. Not after a week. Be prepared.
- **The first 7–10 days may feel worse**. Nausea, anxiety, increased unsteadiness. This is temporary adaptation — ride it out.
- **A full course lasts 6 to 12 months**. Not two weeks. Not a month. A proper course.
- **Prescribed by a doctor** — an evidence-based neurologist or psychiatrist. Self-medication is not acceptable. The dose must be titrated, and discontinuation should be gradual.

### Can You Recover Without Antidepressants?

Yes. Many people recover without SSRIs — through [vestibular exercises](06_vestibular.html), [exposure](12_exposure.html), and anxiety management alone. SSRIs are a tool that **accelerates** the process and takes the edge off. But they're not mandatory.

Think of it like pain relief during physical therapy. You can do it without. But with it — it's more bearable.

## What About Phenibut and Teraligen?

**Phenibut** — a nootropic with mild tranquilizer properties. Popular in Russia and some post-Soviet countries but not approved as a medication in most of the world. Evidence base — weak. Risk of dependence — real. Verdict: not recommended for ongoing PPPD treatment.

**Teraligen** (alimemazine) — an antipsychotic with sedative effects. Sometimes prescribed "for anxiety." But for PPPD, it suppresses vestibular compensation just like benzodiazepines. Plus side effects: drowsiness, weight gain, extrapyramidal disorders.

## Bottom Line: What to Take and What to Skip

| Category | For PPPD | Notes |
|----------|----------|-------|
| **SSRIs / SNRIs** | ✅ Works | The only group with an evidence base |
| **Nootropics** | ❌ Doesn't work | Mexidol, Cavinton, Cerebrolysin — placebo |
| **Benzodiazepines** | ⚠️ Emergency only | Block neuroplasticity, cause dependence |
| **Phenibut** | ❌ Not recommended | Weak evidence, dependence risk |
| **Betahistine** | ❌ Doesn't work | For Ménière's disease, not PPPD |
| **Antipsychotics** | ❌ Not recommended | Inhibit compensation, heavy side effects |
