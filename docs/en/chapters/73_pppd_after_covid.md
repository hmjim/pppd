# PPPD After COVID: Why the Dizziness Won't Go Away After COVID-19

*The coronavirus is behind you. PCR is negative. Lungs are clear. But weeks, months later — you're swaying, the world is floating, your head feels like it's in a fog. You think: "These are long COVID effects." Partially — yes. But what you're feeling right now has a specific name: PPPD.*

---

## What Happened: Three Roads from COVID to PPPD

COVID-19 has been one of the main triggers of PPPD since 2020. There are three mechanisms, and they often work simultaneously:

**1. Direct impact on the vestibular nerve.** The SARS-CoV-2 virus is neurotropic — it can penetrate nerve tissue. In some people, it affects the vestibular nerve or the inner ear. An acute vestibular episode occurs — real vertigo. The brain compensates for it within 2–4 weeks. But if you were panicking at that moment (and who wasn't in 2020?) — PPPD gets triggered.

**2. Anxiety that came along with the illness.** You were lying there with a fever thinking: "What if there are complications? What if it's my lungs? What if it's myocarditis?" News, statistics, stories from people you know — all of this cranked your anxiety to the max. The amygdala memorized: body = danger. And after recovery, it kept scanning every signal.

**3. Post-COVID dysautonomia.** COVID hits the autonomic nervous system. Hence the tachycardia, blood pressure spikes, exercise intolerance, orthostatic hypotension. All of this is the perfect breeding ground for PPPD: the body constantly generates strange signals, the brain reads them as a threat, and triggers the adrenaline loop.

---

## Why Doctors Aren't Helping

You go from neurologist to neurologist, and you hear: "It's post-COVID syndrome, just wait, it'll pass." Or: "Try some Mexidol." Or: "It's VSD" (vegetovascular dystonia — a diagnosis that only exists in Russian-speaking countries and has no international recognition).

The problem is that most neurologists **don't know the abbreviation PPPD**. The ICD-11 code (AB32.0) appeared recently. The doctor sees "dizziness after COVID" — and treats a nonexistent injury, instead of addressing anxiety and neuroplasticity.

If you've had an MRI, Doppler ultrasound, audiogram — and everything is normal — that doesn't mean "everything's fine, just be patient." It means your hardware is fine, but the bug is in the software. And it can be fixed.

---

## How Post-COVID PPPD Differs from "Regular" PPPD

In essence — it doesn't. The mechanism is the same: anxiety → body hyperscanning → adrenaline loop → chronic unsteadiness. The trigger is different (COVID instead of BPPV or stress), but the bug is exactly the same.

There are nuances:

- **Asthenia.** After COVID, weakness often persists for 2–3 months. You confuse it with "severe PPPD" — but these are different things. The weakness will pass; PPPD needs to be treated.
- **POTS (postural tachycardia).** In some post-COVID patients, heart rate jumps by 30+ beats upon standing. This is not panic — this is dysautonomia. You need a cardiology consultation, possibly beta-blockers. POTS is treated in parallel with PPPD.
- **Fear of COVID recurrence.** "What if I get sick again?" — this fear feeds anxiety. It needs to be addressed as a cognitive distortion (catastrophizing).

---

## What to Do Right Now

**Step 1:** Make sure the hardware is fine. Brain MRI, audiogram, basic blood work (ferritin, TSH, vitamin D — deficiencies after COVID are real). If everything is normal — move to step 2.

**Step 2:** Stop treating "long COVID effects." Start treating PPPD. The protocol is the same: vestibular exercises + muscle relaxation + exposure + anxiety work. COVID was the trigger, but it's long gone. What remains is a software bug, and we know how to fix it.

**Step 3:** If you have POTS — see a cardiologist. This is not "anxiety," this is real dysautonomia, and it's treated with medication (ivabradine, propranolol, increased salt and fluid intake).
