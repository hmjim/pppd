# PPPD — ICD-10 and ICD-11 Codes

*"They diagnosed me with VSD." "My chart says osteochondrosis." "The neurologist wrote H81.9." But what's really going on? PPPD has its own code — AB32.0 in ICD-11. Here's why it's not in ICD-10 and what to do about it.*

## PPPD in ICD-11: Code AB32.0

In **ICD-11** (effective since 2022), PPPD has its own dedicated code:

> **AB32.0** — Persistent postural-perceptual dizziness

Section: AB32 — Chronic vestibular syndrome. Category: AB3 — Diseases of vestibular function.

This means PPPD is an **officially recognized medical diagnosis**. Not "nerves." Not "it's all in your head." Not "just take some supplements and rest." The World Health Organization says: this is real. It exists. It's treatable.

## Why There's No PPPD Code in ICD-10

ICD-10 was created in 1990. The diagnostic criteria for PPPD were formulated by the Bárány Society only in **2017** — 27 years later. So in ICD-10, PPPD simply **doesn't exist** as a separate diagnosis.

It's like looking for an article about the iPhone in a 1990 reference book. It hadn't been invented yet.

### What Codes Get Used Instead of PPPD

In practice, doctors use "substitutes" — codes that sort-of fit the description:

| ICD-10 Code | What They Write | The Problem |
|-------------|----------------|-------------|
| **H81.1** | BPPV | PPPD ≠ BPPV, these are completely different diagnoses |
| **H81.4** | Vertigo of central origin | There's no true vertigo in PPPD |
| **H81.9** | Vestibular dysfunction, unspecified | A junk code that tells you nothing |
| **F45.8** | Other somatoform disorders | Used when a psychotherapist makes the diagnosis |
| **R42** | Dizziness and giddiness | That's a symptom code, not a diagnosis |
| **G93.3** | Chronic fatigue syndrome | Sometimes assigned out of sheer desperation |

### Why This Is a Problem

Because PPPD doesn't have its own code in ICD-10:

- **Most doctors don't know about PPPD.** They diagnose "VSD" (a legacy Soviet diagnosis that doesn't exist in ICD-10 or ICD-11), "cervical osteochondrosis," or "dyscirculatory encephalopathy" — diagnoses that either don't exist (VSD) or are not the cause of your dizziness (osteochondrosis).

- **You get ineffective treatment.** Nootropics, "vascular" IV drips, neck massage — all completely off-target. Because the doctor is treating a non-existent disease.

- **PPPD statistics are massively underreported.** The diagnosis "dissolves" into adjacent codes. No research, no funding, no attention.

## When Will Countries Fully Adopt ICD-11?

The transition to ICD-11 has been underway since 2022. In Russia, for example, the Ministry of Health planned to complete the switch by 2025–2027, but the process has been slow — as these things usually are. But sooner or later, code **AB32.0** will become the standard, and it'll be easier for doctors to make the correct diagnosis.

## What to Do Right Now

Don't wait for the healthcare system to catch up. Take action:

**1. If your neurologist doesn't know about PPPD** — print out the Bárány Society diagnostic criteria (2017). Show them code AB32.0 in ICD-11. This isn't rude — it's self-advocacy. You have the right to a correct diagnosis.

**2. If you've been diagnosed with "VSD"** — know this: [VSD is not a diagnosis](72_anxiety_physical_symptoms.html). It doesn't exist in ICD-10 or ICD-11. It's a Soviet-era relic that should have been retired long ago.

**3. If you've been diagnosed with "osteochondrosis"** — know this: cervical osteochondrosis is present in **90% of people over 30**. Including people who feel perfectly fine. Including people who run marathons. Disc bulges on an MRI are a normal age-related finding, not a cause of chronic dizziness.
