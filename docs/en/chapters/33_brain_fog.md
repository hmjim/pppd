# Brain Fog: Why Your Head Feels Like Lead, You Can't Think Straight, and the World Seems Behind Glass

*Your head feels heavy, like it's stuffed with cotton. Thoughts are sluggish, thick as molasses. You read a paragraph and can't remember what it said. The world around you is washed out, muffled, like looking through frosted glass. And the neurologist says: "Everything's normal." Sound familiar? Let's figure out where this fog came from and how to clear it.*

---

## "I'm Getting Dumber"

This is one of the most terrifying complaints. Not pain, not nausea, not unsteadiness — but the feeling that your brain is shutting down. That you're losing yourself.

You're sitting at work and can't write a simple email. Words scatter. You reread one sentence three times — and still don't understand what it's about. A coworker is saying something — and you stare at them thinking: "I hear the words, but I can't assemble them into meaning."

You start googling: "early Alzheimer's," "brain tumor symptoms," "cerebrovascular disease." MRI — clean. Blood vessels — normal. Blood work — perfect. But your head — feels like lead.

Here's what I'll tell you: you're not getting dumber. Your brain isn't breaking. It's **overloaded**. And the fog isn't a malfunction — it's an emergency power-saving mode.

---

## Why Your Head Feels "Stuffed with Cotton": Three Reasons

### 1. Your Brain Is Spending Everything on Balance

Imagine a computer with 200 Chrome tabs open. It barely moves. The cursor lags. Video stutters. Not because the processor burned out — but because all the RAM is taken up.

With PPPD, the same thing happens. Your brain has switched off the balance autopilot and gone to manual control. Now it's spending resources **every single second** to keep you from falling: analyzing visual input, cross-checking vestibular data, monitoring proprioception. For everything else — memory, concentration, speech, analytical thinking — there's not enough processing power left.

You haven't gotten dumber. You just have 95% of your RAM eaten up by the background process "don't fall."

### 2. Cortisol Is Burning Out Your Prefrontal Cortex

When you've been living in anxiety for months, your adrenal glands pump cortisol non-stop. Chronically elevated cortisol hits the **prefrontal cortex** with pinpoint precision — the part of the brain responsible for thinking, planning, and working memory.

Research shows: people with chronic anxiety experience a 20–30% drop in working memory. Not because neurons have died — but because they're suppressed by stress hormones. Remove the stress — clarity returns. This is reversible.

### 3. Derealization — Your Brain's Circuit Breaker

If on top of the fog you feel like the world has become **unreal** — washed out, distant, dreamlike, or like looking through glass — that's a separate mechanism. It's called **derealization**.

Your brain decided: "The boss is on the edge. Anxiety is off the charts. If I don't dial down sensitivity, he'll break." And it turns the brightness of perception down to minimum. Like a circuit breaker in the fuse box — it cuts a circuit so the whole wiring doesn't burn out.

This isn't psychosis. This isn't schizophrenia. It's protection. Unpleasant, scary — but protection. More details — [Derealization in PPPD](40_derealization_pppg.html).

## Myths About Brain Fog

**❌ "It's from poor blood supply to the brain"**
No. A neck vessel ultrasound (Doppler) during cognitive fog from anxiety comes back normal. The brain's blood supply is just fine. The problem isn't in the pipes — it's in the software.

**❌ "It's from cervical osteochondrosis"**
Osteochondrosis is found in 90% of people over 25. It doesn't cause mental fog. It's a radiological finding, not a diagnosis.

**❌ "You need nootropics — to nourish the brain"**
Mexidol, Actovegin, Cerebrolysin, Cortexin — not a single one of them has an evidence base for treating cognitive fog. You can't "nourish" a brain that's overloaded with anxiety. It doesn't need food — it needs relief.

**❌ "I'm losing my mind"**
No. People who are actually losing their minds don't notice anything is wrong. If you're worried about your thinking — your thinking is fine. You're just scared.

## How to Clear the Fog

### 1. Put Balance Back on Autopilot
As long as your brain is spending all its resources on balance — there's no escaping the fog. Vestibular rehabilitation restores the autopilot. The brain frees up RAM — and clarity returns. Exercise program: [Vestibular Rehabilitation](06_vestibular.html).

### 2. Release Your Neck and Scalp
That "helmet" or "band" sensation around your head — that's spasm of the frontal, temporal, and occipital muscles. This muscular armor around your head and neck creates physical pressure. Relieve it: [Jacobson's Progressive Relaxation](05_relaxation.html).

### 3. Stop Monitoring the Fog
Every time you think: "Is the fog here today? Is it bad? What about right now?" — you're burning even more brain resources on a pointless task. It's a vicious cycle: the more you monitor the fog, the thicker it gets.

### 4. Fix Your Sleep
The fog is almost always worse in the morning and after poor sleep. Cortisol peaks in the first hour after waking (the CAR peak). If you're sleeping poorly — the peak hits many times harder. More on sleep and PPPD — [separate chapter](26_sleep.html).

### 5. Check Basic Labs (Once)
Rule out things that can genuinely cause fog: ferritin, vitamin B12, TSH (thyroid), vitamin D. Get them done once, see normal results — and close the topic. Don't retest every month "just in case."

---

## When the Fog Will Lift

The fog isn't a separate disease. It's a **symptom of overload**. It fades as you:
- Put balance back on autopilot (rehabilitation exercises)
- Reduce background anxiety (CBT, exposure)
- Normalize sleep
- Stop monitoring your condition 24/7

For most people, the fog begins to clear within 4–8 weeks of work. First, "windows of clarity" appear — hours when your head feels light and clear. The windows get longer. Then the fog only lingers in the morning. Then it's gone completely.
