# Blood Pressure Spikes from Nerves: Why the Monitor Lies and What Anxiety Has to Do with It

*140/90. You see those numbers and go cold. You measure again — 150/95. Panic: "I have hypertension. Stroke. Crisis." Twenty minutes later — 120/80. An hour later — 135/85. Morning — 115/75. You're bouncing between your GP and a cardiologist, and they say: "Your blood pressure is fine, it's situational." But you don't believe them.*

---

## Why Blood Pressure Jumps

Blood pressure isn't a constant. It changes every minute. A healthy person can be at 110/70 at rest and 140/90 after climbing stairs. That's normal physiology.

With chronic anxiety, adrenaline enters the picture. Here's what it does:

1. **Constricts peripheral blood vessels** — blood pressure rises
2. **Speeds up the heartbeat** — blood pressure rises
3. **Tenses the muscles** (including blood vessel walls) — blood pressure rises

You measured your blood pressure. Saw 140. Got scared. Adrenaline dumped. Blood pressure went to 150. Measured again. 155. The adrenaline loop — the same one described in Chapter 09. Only instead of dizziness — it's the blood pressure monitor.

---

## "White Coat Hypertension" — That's You

In medicine there's a term: **white coat hypertension**. Blood pressure rises when it's being measured — because the very act of measurement triggers anxiety. At home, at rest, at night — blood pressure is normal. But at the doctor's office or during a panic moment — it spikes.

This isn't hypertensive disease. This is an anxiety disorder manifesting through the blood vessels.

Signs that your blood pressure is "from nerves" rather than from hypertension:
- Spikes are tied to stress, anxiety, panic — not consistently elevated
- At rest and during sleep, blood pressure is normal (24-hour ambulatory monitoring — ABPM — will show this)
- No target organ damage (eye fundus, kidneys, heart — all normal)
- Blood pressure normalizes after the anxiety passes

## The Vicious Circle: Blood Pressure → Fear → Blood Pressure

You bought a blood pressure monitor. Now you measure your blood pressure 5 times a day. This is a **checking ritual**, a type of CAS trap. Every measurement is a body scan: "Am I safe?" Every time the numbers are "off" — adrenaline dump → blood pressure rises → another measurement → another dump.

A blood pressure monitor with an anxiety disorder is like Google with hypochondria. The more you check — the worse you feel.

---

## What to Do

### 1. Get an ABPM

Ambulatory Blood Pressure Monitoring. You wear the device for 24 hours, it measures blood pressure automatically — day and night. If the average 24-hour pressure is < 130/80 and nighttime is < 120/70 — you **don't have hypertension**. Period. Do it once — and close this question.

### 2. Put Away the Monitor

Seriously. If the ABPM showed normal results — put the blood pressure monitor in a drawer. Don't sell it — just put it away. Every measurement feeds the anxiety. You don't need daily blood pressure monitoring if you don't have hypertensive disease.

### 3. The Adrenaline Loop — Same Protocol

When you feel like your "blood pressure went up" (heat, pulsing in the temples, anxiety):
- Don't grab the monitor
- Say: "Adrenaline loop. 5 minutes and it'll pass"
- Physiological sigh: double inhale → long exhale
- Wait 5 minutes

The blood pressure will come down on its own — once the adrenaline burns off. It always comes down. That's physiology.

### 4. Muscle Relaxation

Jacobson twice a day. When muscles are relaxed — blood vessels dilate — blood pressure drops. This isn't a metaphor, it's direct physiology. Regular relaxation lowers average daily blood pressure by 5–10 mmHg without medication.

---

## When to Actually See a Doctor

See a cardiologist if:
- Your average 24-hour ABPM reading is > 135/85
- Blood pressure is consistently above 140/90 **at rest** (not during anxiety)
- There's a family history (both parents with hypertension)
- You're 50+ and haven't had your blood pressure checked before

In these cases there may be real hypertension that requires medication. But even with genuine hypertension — anxiety amplifies the spikes, and addressing it is essential.

---

*The blood pressure monitor doesn't show your blood pressure. It shows your anxiety level. And what needs treatment isn't the numbers on the screen — but what's driving them up.*
