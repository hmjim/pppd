# Your Day from Wake-Up to Sleep: The Complete Protocol

*You've read 15 chapters. You've got Jacobson, vestibular exercises, exposure, breathing, meditation, attention focus. A full toolbox. But you sit on the edge of your bed in the morning and think: "So what am I actually supposed to do right now?" Here's the answer — your day, assembled into a single card.*

---

## Morning (The Hardest Time — And That's Normal)

Mornings are when the swaying is worst. This is not a setback and not a worsening. Cortisol peaks between 6 and 8 AM (cortisol awakening response) — the body wakes up in combat-ready mode. Muscles stiffened overnight, proprioception hasn't recalibrated yet. The first 30–60 minutes are the most deceptive: they do NOT show how you actually feel. Don't evaluate the morning. The morning lies.

**07:00 — Wake Up (Without Your Phone)**

Don't grab your smartphone. Don't read the news. Don't check your symptoms. Get up. Slowly. If you're swaying — say: "Cortisol. It'll pass in an hour." Don't evaluate.

**07:05 — Physiological Sigh (30 Seconds)**

Double inhale through the nose → long exhale through the mouth. 3 times. You've switched the nervous system. This isn't a ritual — it's neurophysiology.

**07:10 — Jacobson (10 Minutes)**

Progressive muscle relaxation. Lying down or sitting. Tense → release each muscle group. You're removing the muscle armor that accumulated overnight.

**07:25 — Glass of Water + Breakfast**

Without your phone. Without news. If it's really tough — put on music or a podcast (NOT about health).

---

## First Half of the Day

**08:00 — Leave the House (Exposure, 20–30 Minutes)**

A walk. Not a marathon — just walk. If you're swaying — walk. If you're scared — walk. Exposure only works when it's scary. When it's comfortable — it's a stroll, not training.

Route: familiar, but not too "safe." Not in the park behind your house (too easy), not in a shopping mall at rush hour (too hard to start with). The exposure ladder: one step above your comfort zone.

**During the walk:**
- Focus on the outside world: count red cars, listen to birds, look at treetops
- Do NOT scan your body. If you catch yourself — tag it: "CAS" — and back to the outside world

---

## Midday

**12:00–13:00 — Vestibular Exercises (15 Minutes)**

Exercises from chapter 06. Cawthorne-Cooksey exercises or VOR training. 15 minutes, no more. If the swaying gets worse after the exercises — **that's a good sign**. It means the brain got a workout and is recalibrating. Increased symptoms after training are like muscle soreness after the gym.

**Throughout the day — the 80/20 rule:**

80% of the day, live your normal life. Work, cook, socialize. 20% — focused work on PPPD (exercises, relaxation, reading the book). Don't turn recovery into a full-time job — otherwise PPPD becomes your identity.

---

## Evening

**19:00 — Exercise (30–45 Minutes)**

Cardio: running, cycling, swimming, dancing — whatever gets your heart rate up to 130–150. Exercise isn't just "good for your health." It's a direct tool: it burns off excess cortisol and adrenaline, boosts BDNF (brain-derived neurotrophic factor), literally accelerating neuroplasticity.

No energy for running? Walk. 30 minutes of brisk walking already works.

**21:00 — Journal (5 Minutes)**

Three questions:
1. What did I do today despite my symptoms?
2. Was there a moment when I forgot about PPPD for at least 5 minutes?
3. One thing I'm grateful for.

Don't write about symptoms. Don't analyze your condition. The journal is about life, not illness.

**21:30 — Meditation or NSDR (10–15 Minutes)**

Yoga nidra (NSDR) using the Huberman protocol, or a regular mindfulness meditation. Switches the nervous system into parasympathetic mode before sleep.

**22:00 — Lights Out**

Phone — in another room. Sleep — in a cool, dark room. If you can't fall asleep — don't lie in bed for more than 20 minutes; get up and do something boring, then come back.

---

## "I Don't Have the Energy for All This" — The Minimum Day

There will be days when you're in the pit. No energy for the full program. That's normal. Here's your minimum — 15 minutes that you can do in any state:

- 5 minutes — Jacobson (at least face + shoulders + hands)
- 5 minutes — go outside (even if it's just to the front door and back)
- 5 minutes — write one line in your journal

15 minutes. Even on the darkest day. This isn't a heroic feat — it's the minimum dose that keeps the brain from forgetting the new pathway.
