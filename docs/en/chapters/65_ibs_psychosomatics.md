# IBS and Your Nerves: When Your Gut Hurts but the Gastroenterologist Shrugs

*Stomach in knots every morning. Spasm before an important meeting. Nausea on the subway. Colonoscopy — clean. Ultrasound — normal. Blood work — perfect. And your gut still does whatever it wants. This is IBS — Irritable Bowel Syndrome. And it's from the same playbook as [PPPD](01_what_is_pppg.html).*

## What Is IBS

**Irritable Bowel Syndrome** is a functional gastrointestinal disorder. "Functional" means your gut is physically healthy — but it's not working properly.

Three types:
- **IBS-D** — diarrhea-predominant. Mornings — multiple bathroom sprints. Before any stressor — instant "nervous stomach."
- **IBS-C** — constipation-predominant. Your gut "locks up," belly bloats, gas builds.
- **IBS-M** — mixed. Alternating between constipation and diarrhea. You never know what today's going to bring.

Statistically, IBS affects 10–15% of the world's population. But most people are too embarrassed to talk about it.

## Why Your Gut Reacts to Stress

Your gut has 500 million neurons. That's more than your spinal cord. It's not called the "second brain" for nothing. It's directly connected to the first brain via the vagus nerve (nervus vagus) — a thick cable running from the brainstem to your abdominal organs.

When you're stressed:
1. The amygdala activates "fight-or-flight" mode → adrenaline and cortisol flood your system
2. The sympathetic nervous system takes the wheel
3. Blood gets rerouted from the gut to the muscles (biological logic: "no time for digestion — we need to run from a tiger")
4. Gut motility goes haywire — spasms, speeding up, or shutting down
5. Serotonin (90% of which is produced in the gut!) gets destabilized

That's why your stomach churns before an exam. That's why stress hits you right in the gut. That's why chronic stress turns into chronic IBS.

## The Connection to PPPD and Other Psychosomatic Symptoms

If you have IBS + [dizziness](01_what_is_pppg.html), or IBS + [lump in throat](64_lump_in_throat.html), or IBS + [tachycardia](67_heart_anxiety.html) — you don't have "a bunch of different diseases." You have one overloaded nervous system that's "crashing" on multiple levels at once.

Central sensitization (when the brain amplifies every signal from the body) affects the gut too. Normal peristalsis gets perceived as pain. Ordinary gas feels like "bloating at level 10." The brain hyper-fixates on gut sensations — exactly the way it hyper-fixates on the feeling of unsteadiness in PPPD.

It's the same [adrenaline loop](09_adrenaline_loop.html). Same mechanism. Same treatment.

## "The Only Thing That Helps Is Imodium / Loperamide / Antispasmodics"

Pills knock out the symptom. For half a day. And tomorrow it's back to square one. Because you're treating the effect, not the cause.

IBS can't be fixed with "gut pills." It's fixed by lowering your overall anxiety level — because it's anxiety that's pulling the strings of the vagus nerve.

## What Actually Works for IBS

1. **CBT** — proven effective for IBS (multiple RCTs). [Cognitive Behavioral Therapy](34_cbt_for_dizziness.html) reduces catastrophizing and anxious gut-scanning.

2. **Diaphragmatic breathing** — activates the parasympathetic system through the vagus nerve. 5 minutes of slow breathing (inhale 4 sec, exhale 6–8 sec) directly "calms" the gut.

3. **Progressive muscle relaxation** — [Jacobson's method](05_relaxation.html). Releases the background spasm of intestinal smooth muscle by lowering overall muscle tone.

4. **SSRIs / SNRIs** — work for IBS too (especially IBS-D). Serotonin directly regulates gut motility.

5. **Physical activity** — [exercise](13_sport.html). Regular cardio normalizes peristalsis and lowers cortisol.

6. **Addressing the root causes of anxiety** — [root causes of neurosis](17_root_causes.html), [suppressed emotions](28_suppressed_emotions.html).

## Diet Is Not a Silver Bullet

Yes, the low-FODMAP diet helps reduce bloating and gas. But it addresses the **symptom**, not the **cause**. If you eliminate every trigger food but keep living on your nerves — your gut will still rebel.

Diet is a tactic. Working with your nervous system is the strategy.
