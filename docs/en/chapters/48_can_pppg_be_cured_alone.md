# Can You Cure PPPD on Your Own — and Will It Go Away by Itself?

*The number one question everyone asks: "Do I actually need a doctor, or can I handle this myself?" Here's my honest answer — from personal experience and evidence-based medicine.*

## It Won't Go Away on Its Own. Period.

Let me start with the uncomfortable truth. PPPD is not a cold. A runny nose clears up in a week, even if you do nothing. PPPD plays by different rules.

Remember the [adrenaline loop](09_adrenaline_loop.html)? Symptom → fear → adrenaline → more symptoms. It's a closed cycle. It won't break on its own because you keep feeding it the same reactions every single day.

Here's an analogy. Imagine a program on your computer has frozen. It's eating up all your RAM. You sit there waiting for it to "unfreeze itself." An hour later — nope. A day later — nope. A month later — nope. Because the program is stuck in a loop. Without a reboot — without forced intervention — it will hang there forever.

PPPD is a frozen process in your brain. Passive waiting is like sitting in front of a frozen screen and hoping for a miracle.

In my support community, there are people who lived with PPPD for **10, 15, 30 years**. Not because it's incurable — but because they waited for it to "go away on its own." It didn't.

## But Can You Do It on Your Own?

Now the good news. **Yes, you can.** And what's more — 80% of the work is done by you anyway, even if you're seeing a therapist.

You need a doctor at the start for two things:

**First** — rule out organic causes. Get an MRI, check your hearing, see a neuro-otologist. Make sure it's not a tumor, not multiple sclerosis, not Ménière's disease. A detailed checklist is in the chapter "[Closing the Door on the Clinic](02_medical_checkup.html)."

**Second** — if needed, get started on an SSRI. If your anxiety is so extreme that you can't leave the house, an antidepressant will take the edge off and give you a window to do the work. It's not a "crutch" — it's anesthesia during surgery.

Everything else — **that's daily, independent training of your brain and nervous system**. No doctor can do it for you. No pill will rewire your neural pathways. Only practice.

## What Exactly to Do on Your Own

### 1. Shut Down the Anxiety Scanner

Every time you tune in — "Am I swaying right now? Which leg feels rubbery? Is my head heavy?" — you're pouring gasoline on the fire. Your brain gets the command: "The boss is scanning the body → the threat must be real → keep the defenses up."

This is the [CAS trap](10_cas_trap.html). You need to redirect your attention **to the outside world**. Not to your body. To the color of the walls, to the sounds outside the window, to the smell of coffee. The ATT (Attention Training Technique) is a trainable skill, like riding a bike. Wobbly at first, automatic within a month.

### 2. Vestibular Exercises — Every Day

15–20 minutes a day. Gaze stabilization during head turns. Standing on one leg. Walking with head turns. It's all laid out in the chapter [Vestibular Exercises](06_vestibular.html).

The key principle: **the exercise should cause mild discomfort**. About a 3–4 out of 10. If you're comfortable — you're not training your brain. If it's unbearable — you're overloading it. Find the sweet spot.

### 3. Exposure — Get Outside

Stop hiding at home. Your brain learns safety **only through real experience**. Not through thinking, not through reading articles, not through watching videos. Through physically being in a frightening situation.

Start small. The hallway. The courtyard. The nearest store. Gradually expand your radius. A detailed protocol is in the chapter [Exposure](12_exposure.html) and in the article "[How to Overcome Fear and Walk Alone](50_how_to_walk_alone_agoraphobia.html)."

### 4. Release the Muscle Armor

A tight neck and trapezius muscles — that's not "degenerative disc disease." It's a muscular reaction to chronic stress. Tight neck → distorted signals from the neck to the cerebellum → sensation of unsteadiness. Release the spasm — and you release part of the dizziness. [Jacobson's Progressive Relaxation](05_relaxation.html) is a proven tool.

### 5. Sort Out Your Head

Why did you develop PPPD in the first place? What stress broke you? What [root causes](17_root_causes.html) led to this neurosis? Without answering these questions, you risk curing the symptom — only to have it come back six months later.

## Timeline

I won't sugarcoat it. It won't be quick.

- **Weeks 1–4:** It might get worse. That's normal. Your brain is rewiring, and it doesn't like it. Like the gym: after your first workout, everything aches.
- **Months 1–3:** "Windows" appear — hours or days when symptoms are milder. They get longer.
- **Months 3–6:** Sustained improvement. The unsteadiness is still there, but it no longer runs your life.
- **After 6 months:** Most people return to a full life.

[Setbacks](20_setback_anatomy.html) are a normal part of the process. It's not "everything came back" — it's your brain rebooting.
