# DHI, HADS & Niigata Questionnaires for PPPD: Diagnostic & Severity Scoring

In evidence-based neuro-otology, standardized psychometric and vestibular disability questionnaires are essential for quantifying symptom severity and tracking objective recovery milestones.

Here are the primary validated diagnostic scales used worldwide for PPPD evaluation.

---

## 1. DHI (Dizziness Handicap Inventory)

The DHI is a 25-item self-assessment questionnaire evaluating the multifaceted impact of dizziness across three distinct domains:
* **Emotional Subscale (E):** Frustration, fear of going out, anxiety, depression;
* **Functional Subscale (F):** Interference with vocational duties, domestic tasks, social activities;
* **Physical Subscale (P):** Symptom exacerbation triggered by head motion, quick turns, bending down.

### DHI Score Stratification:
* **0–30 points:** Mild handicap (favorable baseline compensation).
* **31–60 points:** Moderate handicap (active vestibular and behavioral rehabilitation required).
* **61–100 points:** Severe handicap (significant impairment requiring comprehensive multi-modal protocol).

---

## 2. HADS (Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale)

A 14-item validated screening instrument divided into two 7-item subscales:
* **0–7 points:** Non-clinical range (Normal).
* **8–10 points:** Subclinical borderline mood/anxiety disturbance.
* **11+ points:** Clinically significant anxiety or depressive state contributing to PPPD chronification.

---

## 3. Niigata PPPD Questionnaire (NPQ)

Developed by specialized neuro-otologists at Niigata University, the NPQ is a 12-item instrument designed specifically for PPPD profiling across three core symptom triggers:
1. **Upright Posture:** Exacerbation while standing or walking;
2. **Active/Passive Motion:** Exacerbation by self-movement or traveling in vehicles;
3. **Visual Stimulation:** Exacerbation in complex visual environments (supermarkets, bright displays, moving patterns).
