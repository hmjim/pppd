# PPPD and Dizziness — Why Your Head Is Constantly Spinning

*You feel unsteady. Every day. You wake up — and you're already swaying. You walk to the kitchen — the floor is "floating." You step outside — your legs are like cotton wool. All your tests come back normal. But the dizziness — it's very real. Let's figure out what's going on.*

## Dizziness in PPPD — It's Not What You Think

When someone says "I'm dizzy," you usually picture a carousel — the room is spinning, the walls are moving, you can't stand up. That's **true rotational vertigo**. With PPPD, that's usually not what happens.

Dizziness in PPPD is different. It's quiet, background, and exhausting:

- **Swaying** — like being on a ship in calm waters. Not a storm — a gentle rocking. But constant.
- **"Floating"** — the ground under your feet feels liquid. Soft. Unstable.
- **Wobbling** — the world slightly "trembles," like you're looking through heat haze.
- **Unsteadiness** — your legs feel "not your own," the floor drops away, you're being pulled to one side.

This is **non-spinning dizziness**. No rotation, no nystagmus, you don't fall. But it feels no less agonizing than a real storm. It's just that a storm is 10 minutes of terror. PPPD is quiet torture for months on end.

## Why You Feel Dizzy with PPPD

Your brain receives information about your body's position from three "sensors":

1. **Eyes** — they see where up is, where down is, where the horizon is
2. **Vestibular system** (inner ear) — it detects tilts, turns, acceleration
3. **Proprioception** — muscles and joints tell the brain where every body part is

Normally, these three data streams match up. The brain automatically cross-references them — and you stand straight without even thinking about it.

In PPPD, this process breaks down. Not because the "sensors are broken" — all three work fine. The problem is in **central processing**. The brain gets stuck in hypervigilance mode and starts "double-checking" every signal. Like an antivirus scanning every single file — and the computer freezes, even though the files are perfectly fine.

### The Breakdown Chain

Chronic stress → cortisol release → [muscle armor](04_muscle_armor.html) → neck tension → **distorted proprioceptive signals** from neck muscles to the cerebellum → desynchronization of the three "sensors" → the brain can't build a stable picture → the sensation of dizziness.

Plus: the brain switches to [visual control](08_visual_dependence.html) of balance — "I don't trust the vestibular system, I'll rely only on my eyes." But the eyes can't handle this load, especially in visually busy spaces (stores, malls, the subway). Hence — things get worse in visually stimulating environments.

## How PPPD Differs from Other Types of Dizziness

Not every "I'm dizzy" means PPPD. Here are the key differences:

| Feature | PPPD | BPPV | Meniere's Disease | Vestibular Neuritis |
|---------|------|------|-------------------|-------------------|
| **Type** | Non-spinning (swaying, unsteadiness) | Spinning ("carousel") | Spinning | Spinning |
| **Duration** | Hours–days–months (background) | 5–60 seconds when turning head | 20 min – 12 hours | Days–weeks |
| **Trigger** | Stress, visual load, upright position | Head turn, rolling over in bed | Spontaneous | Spontaneous |
| **Hearing** | Normal | Normal | Reduced in one ear | Normal |
| **MRI** | Normal | Normal | Normal | Normal |
| **Treatment** | [CBT](34_cbt_for_dizziness.html) + [vestibular rehab](06_vestibular.html) + [exposure](12_exposure.html) | Epley maneuver (1–3 sessions) | Medication | Rehab + time |

**Important:** PPPD often develops **after** BPPV or vestibular neuritis. The episode has passed, but the brain "remembers" the sensation and keeps reproducing it. This is the [adrenaline loop](09_adrenaline_loop.html) — the brain gets "stuck" on the memory of the episode. More details: [BPPV vs. PPPD — what's the difference](54_dppg_vs_pppg_difference.html).

## When Dizziness Gets Worse

Typical triggers in PPPD:

- **Upright position** — standing and walking are worse, lying down is easier. Because standing forces the brain to process more data.
- **Morning** — after sleep the brain is "recalibrating," the first 30–60 minutes are the foggiest.
- **Stores, malls, the subway** — [visual dependence](08_visual_dependence.html) overloads the brain with the riot of shelves and the flicker of passing people.
- **Stress and anxiety** — any emotional tension increases unsteadiness. Had an argument with someone — an hour later the swaying is twice as bad.
- **Fatigue and sleep deprivation** — the brain is already maxed out, without sleep it just "crashes." [Sleep and PPPD](26_sleep.html) are tightly linked.
- **Phone and screens** — scrolling through a feed is rapid visual motion that overloads a [visually-dependent brain](08_visual_dependence.html).

## How to Treat Dizziness in PPPD

PPPD is a **fully reversible condition**. The dizziness goes away. Not in 3 days — but within 2–6 months with systematic effort.

1. **[Vestibular rehabilitation](06_vestibular.html)** — "retraining" the brain to process balance signals properly. Every day, 15–20 minutes.
2. **[Exposure therapy](12_exposure.html)** — gradually returning to scary situations. The brain learns safety only through experience.
3. **[Relaxation](05_relaxation.html)** — releasing the [muscle armor](04_muscle_armor.html) that fuels dizziness through a locked-up neck.
4. **Working with anxiety** — [metacognitive therapy](15_metacognition.html), [cognitive distortions](16_cognitive_distortions.html). Lower the anxiety — lower the unsteadiness.
5. **[Exercise](13_sport.html)** — physical activity reduces cortisol and stimulates [neuroplasticity](14_neuroplasticity.html).
