# PPPD Symptoms — The Complete Checklist

*A detailed list of every PPPD symptom. If you recognize yourself in at least 3–4 of these — you're in the right place. And if it's 7–8 — you're definitely in the right place.*

## 5 Diagnostic Criteria for PPPD (Bárány Society, 2017)

The official criteria a doctor uses to diagnose PPPD. All five must be met:

1. **A sensation of unsteadiness, wobbliness, or non-spinning dizziness** — present for **3 months or more**. Not in attacks — as a background. Like the hum of an air conditioner you no longer notice, but it's running all the time.
2. **Symptoms are present most of the day** — at least 15 days per month. They may wax and wane — but the background remains.
3. **Worsened by upright posture, movement, and visual stimulation** — worse standing than lying down. Worse in a store than at home. On the subway — absolute hell.
4. **Onset is preceded by a triggering event** — BPPV, vestibular neuritis, a panic attack, severe stress, trauma. Something "clicked" — and it all started.
5. **Symptoms are not better explained by another condition** — organic causes have been ruled out by examinations (MRI, Doppler ultrasound, audiometry — all normal).

## Core PPPD Symptoms

### Unsteadiness and Instability
- A sensation of a "floating floor" — as if you're walking on the deck of a ship in calm waters
- "Cotton-wool" or "drunk" legs — not weakness, but the **sensation** of wobbliness
- A feeling that the ground is dropping away beneath you — especially in wide-open spaces (a plaza, a parking lot)
- Swaying while standing still — you're being pulled, as if someone is pushing you
- Worse in the morning — after sleep the brain hasn't had time to "recalibrate" your balance, the first hour is the foggiest
- More details: [Unsteady when walking — why your legs feel like cotton wool](32_unsteadiness.html)

### Dizziness
- Not spinning (not a carousel), but **swaying, floating, wobbling**
- Worse in an upright position — standing and walking are worse, lying down is easier
- Triggered by head turns and bending over
- A sensation of "sinking" while walking — as if the floor is "giving way"
- More details: [Dizziness in PPPD](37_pppg_dizziness.html)

### Visual Symptoms
- A "floating" image before your eyes — the world is "out of focus"
- Discomfort from flickering lights, screens, scrolling
- Gets worse in visually busy environments — stores, malls, the subway. Colorful shelves are torture
- [Visual dependence](08_visual_dependence.html) — the brain over-relies on vision because it "doesn't trust" the vestibular system

### Cognitive Symptoms
- [Brain fog](33_brain_fog.html) — "cotton wool," heaviness, sluggishness. You read a paragraph three times and still don't get it
- Difficulty concentrating — the brain is busy "maintaining balance," there's no processing power left for thinking
- [Derealization](27_depersonalization.html) — the world feels "like you're behind glass," "like a movie"
- Absent-mindedness, forgetfulness — "where was I going? why did I walk into this room?"

### Muscular Symptoms
- Chronic neck and shoulder tension — [muscle armor](04_muscle_armor.html). Your neck is like stone
- Jaw clenching (bruxism) — grinding teeth at night, morning pain in the temples
- Neck and back-of-head pain — not "osteochondrosis," but muscle spasm from stress
- A sensation of a "band" around your head — a pressing tension headache

### Anxiety Symptoms
- Fear of falling, of losing consciousness — "what if I collapse in the middle of the street?"
- [Panic attacks](68_panic_attacks.html) — the [adrenaline loop](09_adrenaline_loop.html) at full throttle
- Hyper-fixation on bodily sensations — "is my heart racing? does my head feel heavy?"
- Compulsive body "scanning" — the [CAS trap](10_cas_trap.html)
- Avoiding scary places — you stop going to the store, stop riding the subway, stop going out alone

## What Is NOT a Symptom of PPPD

If you have any of the following — get further testing:

- **Loss of consciousness** (fainting) — with PPPD you don't faint. Ever. If you lose consciousness — it's not PPPD
- **True spinning vertigo** (episodes lasting 5–60 seconds when turning your head) — that's more likely [BPPV](54_dppg_vs_pppg_difference.html)
- **Hearing loss in one ear** — may indicate Meniere's disease
- **Double vision** — a neurological issue, needs an MRI
- **Speech or swallowing difficulties** — requires urgent evaluation

## PPPD Is Fully Reversible

Every symptom listed above is a manifestation of a functional glitch, not structural damage. The hardware is fine. It's the software that's bugging out. With the right approach ([PPPD treatment](30_treatment_overview.html), [vestibular rehabilitation](06_vestibular.html), [CBT](34_cbt_for_dizziness.html), [exposure therapy](12_exposure.html)), symptoms resolve within 2–6 months.
