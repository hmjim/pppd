# Psychosomatic dizziness: symptoms, causes and treatments 

*When the tests are normal, but your head is spinning - what is psychogenic dizziness and how to cope with it.*

---

## What is psychosomatic (psychogenic) dizziness 

**Psychosomatic dizziness** is a term that is often used in everyday life to describe functional vestibular disorders (primarily **PPD** and anxiety disorders). 

The main difference: a person **does not have organic damage** to the brain, blood vessels or inner ear, but the physical sensation of unsteadiness, lightheadedness and a “floating world” is absolutely real. 

## Symptoms of psychogenic dizziness 

- Feeling of rocking, unsteadiness, “instability” in the body 
- Increased discomfort during stress, anxiety, fatigue 
- Feeling of “unreality” of the surrounding world (derealization) 
- Cotton-heavy head, difficult to concentrate 
- Lack of true rotation of objects around (unlike BPPV or Meniere's disease) 
- Increases in crowded places, supermarkets, open spaces 

## Why psychosomatic dizziness occurs 

1. **Chronic stress.** The autonomic nervous system is in hypertonicity, causing spasms of the neck muscles and distortion of proprioceptive signals. 
2. **Failure of filtering sensory information.** The brain stops filtering background micro-vibrations of the body and perceives them as a threat of falling. 
3. **Panic attacks and phobic instability.** The fear of a repeat attack of dizziness itself provokes new symptoms. 

## What treatment helps 

- **Vestibular gymnastics** for retraining sensory systems ([more](06_vestibular.html)) 
- **Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT)** to eliminate the fear of falling 
- **Body practices and relaxation** ([progressive relaxation according to Jacobson](05_relaxation.html)) 
- **Pharmacotherapy (SSRIs)** - as prescribed by a doctor with a pronounced anxiety component
