# How to Treat Psychosomatics: A Step-by-Step Recovery Plan

*You've already figured out that your symptoms are psychosomatic. Your tests are normal. Junk diagnoses have been thrown out. There's only one question left: what do you do now? Here's a concrete protocol.*

## The Golden Rule: There's No Separate Treatment for Each Symptom

If you simultaneously feel [unsteady](32_unsteadiness.html), have [pressure in your throat](64_lump_in_throat.html), [a pounding heart](67_heart_anxiety.html), and a churning stomach — you don't need to treat four different diseases with four different doctors.

It's one problem: **an overloaded nervous system**. One source — one treatment.

Think of an electrical panel. A power surge tripped five breakers: lights, outlets, water heater, stove, washing machine. You don't fix each appliance separately. You stabilize the voltage — and all the breakers reset.

Psychosomatics is treated by lowering the tension in your nervous system. When the system calms down — the symptoms go away on their own.

## The Protocol: 5 Pillars

### 1. Stop Scanning Your Body

This is the first and most important step.

Every time you tune in — "is my heart racing? am I dizzy? is my throat tight?" — you activate the [CAS trap](10_cas_trap.html). The brain gets the signal: "The boss is looking for a threat → the threat must be real → activate anxiety → amplify symptoms."

What to do: **redirect your attention to the external world**. Not to your body. To the sounds outside the window. The color of the walls. A coworker's conversation. The texture of the keyboard under your fingers.

The ATT (Attention Training Technique) by Adrian Wells is a trainable skill. For the first few days, your brain will fight you on it. After 2–3 weeks, the redirection becomes automatic.

### 2. Release Muscle Tension

Chronic stress = chronic muscle spasm. [Muscle armor](04_muscle_armor.html) is the physical foundation of psychosomatics. Locked-up neck → dizziness. Locked-up throat → lump. Locked-up diaphragm → shortness of breath. Locked-up abdomen → IBS.

The tool: [Jacobson's progressive muscle relaxation](05_relaxation.html). 20 minutes a day. Every day. You're training your brain to issue the "relax" command — and the body starts obeying.

### 3. Break the Adrenaline Loop

The [adrenaline loop](09_adrenaline_loop.html) is the engine of psychosomatics. Symptom → fear → adrenaline → more symptoms → more fear.

How to break it:

- **Paradoxical intention** (Viktor Frankl) — instead of "please make it stop," say: "Go ahead, heart — pound harder. Show me what you've got." Remove the fear from the equation — and the loop stalls.
- **[Exposure](12_exposure.html)** — stop avoiding the situations that scare you. Every avoidance = confirmation to the brain that the danger is real.
- **[Cognitive restructuring](16_cognitive_distortions.html)** — replace catastrophic thoughts with realistic ones. "My heart is pounding → must be a heart attack" → "My heart is pounding → it's adrenaline. It'll pass in 5 minutes."

### 4. Get Your Body Moving

[Exercise](13_sport.html) is one of the most powerful tools for psychosomatics. And the most underrated.

- **Cardio** (running, swimming, cycling) — "burns off" adrenaline and cortisol. Lowers your baseline anxiety level.
- **Strength training** — builds proprioception, gives a sense of control over your body.
- **Stretching / yoga** — lengthens chronically spasmed muscles.

30–40 minutes, 4–5 times a week. Start with walking if even a stroll feels like an achievement right now.

### 5. Get to the Root Cause

Symptoms are smoke. The fire is inside.

Why did you end up with psychosomatics? What stress pushed you to the breaking point? What [root causes](17_root_causes.html) led to the overload?

Maybe you spent years stuffing down your [emotions](28_suppressed_emotions.html). Maybe you're living a life that doesn't fit you. Maybe you've been [playing the "nice person" role](60_good_person_syndrome.html) — and your body is footing the bill.

Without answering these questions, you risk clearing the symptoms — only to have them come back in six months. Or new ones in their place.

## SSRIs — If You Need Support

[SSRIs](51_medications_for_pppg.html) (Sertraline, Escitalopram) are the only class of medications with an evidence base for functional disorders. They work not just for PPPD — but also for IBS, cardiophobia, and chronic pain.

SSRIs aren't mandatory. Many people recover without them. But if your anxiety is so severe that you can't function — an antidepressant opens a window for doing the work. Prescribed by a doctor.

## How Long Will This Take

Honestly:
- **1 month** — first changes. Muscles start to relax. Sleep improves.
- **2–3 months** — noticeable symptom reduction. "Windows" (symptom-free periods) get longer.
- **4–6 months** — sustained improvement. Symptoms are still there, but they're background noise — not the center of your life.
- **6–12 months** — recovery. Symptoms are gone. You're living a normal life.

[Setbacks](20_setback_anatomy.html) will happen. That's normal. It's not "everything came back" — it's your brain rebooting.

## Start Today

Not "next Monday." Not "when I'm in the right mood." Today.

Do three things right now:
1. Put your hands on the table. Feel the texture of the surface. You just redirected your attention outward.
2. Tense and relax your shoulders. 5 seconds of tension — 10 seconds of relaxation. Repeat 3 times.
3. Take 5 slow breaths. Inhale for 4 counts, exhale for 6.

Congratulations — you've started.
