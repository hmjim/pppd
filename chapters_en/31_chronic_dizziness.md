# Constant dizziness: reasons, when it is PPPD and what to do 

*Have you been shaking for weeks or months, doctors shrug their shoulders, but your tests are normal? Most likely it's PPPD - and it's treatable.*

---

## Why do I always feel dizzy? 

If dizziness lasts **more than 3 months**, it is no longer “nerves” or the consequences of a cold. This is a chronic condition that has a specific name: **PPV** - persistent postural-perceptual dizziness. 

PPPD is the second most common reason for visits to otoneurologists. Diagnosed in **15–20% of patients** in vestibular clinics. However, most people go from doctor to doctor for years without receiving a correct diagnosis. 

## What kind of dizziness happens: PPPD vs other types 

Not all dizziness is PPPD. Here's how to tell the difference: 

###PPV (persistent postural perceptual dizziness) 
- Feeling of **wobbly, swaying, instability** - not rotating 
- Lasts **hours, days, weeks** continuously 
- Worsens while standing and walking, easier while lying down 
- Intensifies in colorful spaces (shops, subway, crowd) 
- All tests and MRI are **normal** 
- Often accompanied by anxiety 

### BPPV (benign positional) 
- **Sharp rotation** for 10–60 seconds 
- Occurs when turning the head in bed 
- Treated in 1–2 sessions with the Epley maneuver 

### Vestibular migraine 
- Attacks of dizziness + **headache** 
- Lasts minutes to hours, then passes 
- Treated with migraine prophylaxis 

### Meniere's disease 
- Attacks of rotation + **tinnitus** + hearing loss 
- Lasts 20 minutes – several hours 
- Characteristic changes on the audiogram 

## Constant unsteadiness when walking: why are your legs “wobbly” 

The most common complaint with PPPD is **“wobbles when walking”**. This happens because the brain has switched to **visual control** of balance, turning off the vestibular system and proprioception. 

When you walk, the visual image is constantly moving. The brain interprets this movement as instability - and you feel “cotton wool” in your legs, a sensation of a floating floor, rocking. 

More details about the mechanism: [What is PPPD - symptoms, causes](01_what_is_pppg.html). 

## Head fog due to dizziness 

If, in addition to unsteadiness, you feel **"fog in the head"** - heaviness, vagueness, inability to concentrate - this is a typical accompanying symptom of PPPD. 

Reasons: 
- **Brain overload** - it spends all resources maintaining balance 
- **Chronic anxiety** - increased cortisol reduces cognitive function 
- **Derealization** - a protective mechanism of the brain during extreme stress 

The fog goes away as PPPD is treated. There is no need to treat it separately. 

## What to do if you constantly feel dizzy

### Step 1: Rule out organic causes 
Undergo basic examinations: MRI of the brain, ultrasound examination of neck vessels, examination by an otoneurologist, audiometry. A detailed checklist is in the chapter [What examinations to undergo](02_medical_checkup.html). 

### Step 2: Accept the diagnosis of PPPV 
If the examination is normal, it is PPPD. Not a “mystery disease.” Not “psychosomatics that you can’t do anything about.” A specific diagnosis with a specific treatment protocol. 

### Step 3: Start comprehensive treatment 
Five areas that work for PPPD: 
1. Vestibular gymnastics ([exercises](06_vestibular.html)) 
2. Cognitive behavioral therapy 
3. Exposure to frightening situations 
4. Regular physical activity 
5. SSRIs (as prescribed by a doctor) 

Complete Guide: [PPG Treatment](30_treatment_overview.html). 

### Step 4: Relieve Muscle Tension 
PPPD is always accompanied by chronic muscle spasm. Jacobson relaxation is a proven technique for relieving it. [Step-by-step instructions](05_relaxation.html). 

## Frequently asked questions 

**“I’m 25/30/40 years old. Can I have PPPD at my age?”** 
Yes. PPPV most often begins between the ages of 20 and 50, often after stress, a panic attack, or a history of BPPV. 

**“I’m dizzy from stress. Is this PPPD?”** 
If dizziness persists after a stressful event for more than 3 months, this is one of the criteria for PPPD. Stress is a trigger, not a cause. 

**“Can PPPD go away on its own?”** 
In rare cases, yes, if the stress level decreases naturally. But more often, without targeted work, PPPD becomes chronic. 

**“Osteochondrosis / VAD (autonomic dysfunction) - the cause of my dizziness?”** 
No. Osteochondrosis occurs in 90% of people over 30 years of age and does not cause chronic dizziness. VAD (autonomic dysfunction) is an outdated diagnosis that is not included in international classifications.
