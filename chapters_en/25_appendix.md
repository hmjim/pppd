## Appendix A. Emergency Pocket Card (Save to Your Phone)

*Read this out loud or silently if a sudden wave of anxiety or unsteadiness hits you while you're out in public:*

1. **STOP.** Plant your feet right where you are. Don't run. Stopping sends a direct physiological signal to your brain: *"There is no danger here—we aren't fleeing a predator."*
2. **THIS IS ADRENALINE.** Your heart rate spiked and your vision started swimming because your adrenal glands just dumped a surge of hormones into your bloodstream. That is normal human chemistry. It will **burn off completely in 3–5 minutes**—as long as you don't throw fuel on the fire with catastrophic thoughts. Wait it out.
3. **BOX BREATHING:** Inhale (4 seconds) ➔ hold (4 seconds) ➔ exhale (4 seconds) ➔ hold (4 seconds). Complete 5 slow cycles to rebalance your oxygen and carbon dioxide levels.
4. **5-4-3-2-1 GROUNDING:**
    * 👁 Spot **any 5 blue or green objects** around you.
    * ✋ Notice **4 physical sensations** (the pressure of your soles inside your shoes, the air against your cheeks, your fingers gripping your keys or phone).
    * 👂 Pick out **3 distinct sounds** (the hum of traffic, a bird, a passing conversation).
    * 👃 Catch **any 2 scents** (coffee, asphalt, the collar of your jacket).
    * 👅 Focus on **1 taste** (take a sip of water).
5. **EYES ON THE HORIZON:** Lift your chin. Lock your gaze onto a distant building or tree. Do not stare down at your feet!
6. **PARADOXICAL SURRENDER:** Speak straight to the fear: *"Go ahead—rock me even harder. I give this permission to be here. I'm standing on solid ground, and I am safe."*

## Appendix C. How to Measure Your Progress (HADS and DHI Scales)

To keep your anxious brain from discounting how far you've come, translate your recovery into hard numbers.

### 1. Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale (HADS)
* **Normal range:** 0–7 points.
* **Subclinical / borderline anxiety or depression:** 8–10 points.
* **Clinically significant anxiety or depression:** 11 points and above.
* *Your goal:* Bring your anxiety score below 8 points.

### 2. Dizziness Handicap Inventory (DHI)
* **Mild handicap:** 0–30 points.
* **Moderate handicap:** 31–60 points.
* **Severe handicap:** 61–100 points.
* *Your goal:* Reduce your DHI score by 18 points or more while working through this book (the validated threshold for clinically meaningful improvement).

## Appendix F. Glossary of Key Terms

| Term | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- |
| **PPPD** | Persistent Postural-Perceptual Dizziness (ICD-11: AB32.0) |
| **Amygdala** | Almond-shaped threat center in the brain that triggers the fight-or-flight response |
| **CNS** | Central Nervous System |
| **Autonomic Nervous System (ANS)** | The branch of the nervous system that regulates automatic bodily processes (heart rate, vascular tone, digestion) |
| **Sympathetic** | The mobilization branch of the ANS (accelerates heart rate, constricts blood vessels) |
| **Parasympathetic** | The rest-and-recovery branch of the ANS (slows heart rate, restores baseline calm) |
| **Cortisol** | Primary stress hormone secreted by the adrenal glands |
| **Adrenaline** | Acute fight-or-flight hormone; metabolizes and burns off in 3–5 minutes |
| **Oxytocin** | The "bonding hormone"—a natural cortisol antagonist released during connection and self-compassion |
| **VOR** | Vestibulo-Ocular Reflex—the neural link between the inner ear balance organs and eye muscles |
| **CAS** | Cognitive Attentional Syndrome—the brain's "internal scanner" (rumination + constant symptom monitoring) |
| **CBT** | Cognitive Behavioral Therapy |
| **MCT** | Metacognitive Therapy (developed by Adrian Wells) |
| **PRT** | Pain Reprocessing Therapy (developed by Alan Gordon) |
| **CBT Thought Record (ABC)** | Structured diary: Situation → Thought → Emotion → Response |
| **DHI** | Dizziness Handicap Inventory—clinical scale measuring functional impact of dizziness |
| **HADS** | Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale—validated screening scale for anxiety and depression |
| **Proprioception** | Your sense of body position in space (mechanoreceptors in muscles, joints, and the soles of your feet) |
| **Derealization** | A protective stress response where the surrounding world feels unreal ("behind glass," "in a fog") |
| **Optic Flow** | The streaming motion of visual scenery across the retina as you move through space |
| **Neuroplasticity** | The brain's ability to physically rewire and build new neural pathways |
| **SSRIs** | Selective Serotonin Reuptake Inhibitors (antidepressants also used to calm vestibular hyperexcitability) |
| **BDNF** | Brain-Derived Neurotrophic Factor—biological "fertilizer" that supports neuron growth and rewiring |
