# Nervous Shortness of Breath: Why You Can't Get Enough Air Even Though Your Lungs Are Healthy

*You take a breath — and it feels like the air doesn't reach. As if your lungs only filled halfway. You try to inhale deeper — it doesn't work. You start yawning to "get a breath in" — doesn't help. Your chest tightens. Your throat clenches. You run to the window. You check the pulse oximeter: saturation 98%. Lungs are fine. But you're suffocating.*

---

## Hyperventilation Syndrome: You're Breathing Too Much, Not Too Little

The paradox: it feels like there's too little air — but in reality you're breathing **too much**. With chronic anxiety, breathing becomes rapid and shallow — you don't even notice it. This washes CO₂ (carbon dioxide) out of your blood.

When CO₂ drops — respiratory alkalosis occurs: the blood becomes too alkaline. And here's what starts:

- **Cerebral vasospasm** — hence the brain fog, dizziness, derealization
- **Feeling of air hunger** — even though there's more than enough oxygen in your blood
- **Numbness in hands and face** — tingling in the fingers, "pins and needles" around the mouth
- **Lump in the throat** — the diaphragm is in spasm, the throat is clenched
- **Tachycardia** — the heart compensates for the change in blood pH

See the pattern? These aren't three different diseases. This is **one mechanism** — excessive breathing driven by anxiety. And it's directly linked to PPPD: hyperventilation intensifies dizziness, dizziness intensifies anxiety, anxiety intensifies hyperventilation. A vicious circle.

---

## Why the Diaphragm "Turns to Stone"

Under stress, the body braces for impact — and tenses up the muscles. One of the first casualties is the diaphragm. The main breathing muscle turns into a board. Instead of deep diaphragmatic breathing, you breathe with the "tops" of your lungs — with your chest and shoulders. That's enough for survival, but the brain decides there's "not enough air."

Add the muscle armor of the neck and traps (Chapter 04) — and you get the full package: neck is rock-hard, throat is clenched, diaphragm isn't moving, breathing is shallow, CO₂ drops, head spins, and you feel like you're dying.

---

## "What If It's Asthma?"

If you have real asthma — you know. Asthma = wheezing, shortness of breath on exhale (not inhale), attacks are relieved by an inhaler. With nervous shortness of breath — the pulse oximeter shows 96–99%, chest X-ray is clear, spirometry is normal. The allergist-pulmonologist will say: "Your lungs are healthy."

If you've already heard that — then the problem isn't your lungs. The problem is in the software that controls your breathing. And that software can be rewritten.

---

## How to Fix Your Breathing

### 1. Stop Trying to "Breathe Deeper"

Deep breaths are a trap. You're already breathing too much. Every panicked inhale drops your CO₂ even further. Instead:

**Focus on the EXHALE.** A long, slow exhale. The inhale will take care of itself — it's a reflex, you don't need to control it. Exhale 6–8 seconds → pause 2 seconds → the inhale will come on its own.

### 2. "Box" Breathing (Box Breathing)

- Inhale — 4 seconds
- Hold — 4 seconds
- Exhale — 4 seconds
- Hold — 4 seconds
- 5 cycles

The hold after inhaling raises CO₂ → blood vessels dilate → head clears → panic retreats.

### 3. Unlocking the Diaphragm

Lie on your back. Place one hand on your chest, the other on your belly. Breathe so that ONLY the hand on your belly moves. Chest — stays still. This is diaphragmatic breathing. 5 minutes morning and evening. After 2 weeks the diaphragm will start to "wake up" and your breathing will become deeper automatically.

---

## The Link to PPPD

If you have PPPD + shortness of breath — these aren't two separate diagnoses. It's one mechanism: chronic anxiety → muscle armor → hyperventilation → dizziness. Treat the PPPD — and the shortness of breath goes away too. The recovery system is the same: vestibular rehabilitation + relaxation + exposure + anxiety management.

---

*You're not suffocating. You're over-breathing. And the paradoxical prescription is to breathe LESS, not more. A long exhale is the reset button for your nervous system.*
