# New personality and architecture of boundaries: who am I without my illness? 

*The existential impasse of recovery, why the old personality led to neurosis and how to build boundaries to protect your health.*

---

## 1. Essence and issues: the existential vacuum of recovery 

When the symptoms of PPPD finally begin to subside, many patients are suddenly faced with an unexpected and frightening problem - an **existential crisis**. 

The disease took up 80% of your time and thoughts. You woke up with the thought of being unsteady, planned your day based on your symptoms, discussed your health with loved ones, googled and sat on forums. Illness was your main occupation, your topic of conversation and your identity. You are used to being “the one who is sick and trying to get better.” 

And so, when the pitching goes away, a ringing emptiness forms inside. A terrible, unconscious question arises: * “Who am I without my illness? What should I talk to people about now? How to fill the free 8 hours of attention a day? Who am I now? 

It turns out that being healthy is unusual and even scary. The brain is afraid of this emptiness and may unconsciously try to bring the symptoms back, just to return to the familiar, understandable rut of the “struggle for survival.” 

## 2. Physiological explanation: neural identity shift and the cost of polite silence 

Recovery from neurosis is not a return to your “old self.” This is physically impossible. 

That old personality of yours - with all its perfectionism, inability to refuse, habit of tolerating toxic people, sacrificing oneself for the sake of other people's interests and silently swallowing grievances - led you to a dead end where the fuses of the autonomic system burned out. If you try to recover and remain the same reliable “comfortable person”, your nervous system will very quickly return you to PPPD. 

At the level of neurophysiology, boundaries are a mechanism for protecting your central nervous system from overload. 

Every time you accept overtime work you don't want to do, or listen to an hour-long complaint from a toxic friend, your amygdala becomes aroused. You suppress anger and irritation (because you “have to be good”). Suppressed emotions cause muscle spasms in the neck and diaphragm. Neck spasm cuts off normal blood flow and receptor signals ➔ hello, dizziness. 

Building strict personal boundaries is not selfishness, it is **hygiene of your autonomic system**. Your boundaries are a fence around your “video card” that prevents it from overheating from other people’s tasks. 

## 3. Cognitive Traps: “If I say no, I will be rejected.” 

Main cognitive trap: * “I have to justify myself when I refuse. If I just say “no” without a long explanation, people will think I’m selfish, stop loving me and turn away.”*

Attempts to soften the refusal lead to the fact that you begin to come up with complex excuses, lie, or end up agreeing, betraying yourself. Remember: **your “no” has value in itself**. You don't need a neurologist's certificate to avoid going to painful family gatherings. You don't have to make excuses to your boss for not working on your legal days off. If people only value you when you are “convenient” and fulfill their requests at the expense of your health, then this is not a relationship, this is exploitation. 

## 4. Point of Support: Insight 

>You can't get better by staying the same. The person you were led you to neurosis. Recovery is not about mending an old fence. This is the demolition of an old rotten house and the construction of a new building with strong walls and locks on the doors. Learn to close doors.

## 5. Practice: Setting strict vegetative boundaries 

We move on to the practice of building a new identity and protecting our resources. 

### Step 1. Declaration of boundaries 
Write a list of 4 strict rules that you introduce to protect your nervous system from overload. Remember their wording: 
1. **Professional boundaries:** *“I do not work on weekends and do not respond to work messages after 19:00”*. 
2. **Family boundaries:** *“I do not go to events that are physically draining and cause anxiety, even if relatives will be offended”*. 
3. **Information boundaries:** *“I don’t listen to other people’s toxic complaints about life for more than 10 minutes. My resource is limited."* 
4. **Disease Limits:** *“I no longer discuss my symptoms and frailty with loved ones every day. This feeds my neurosis.”* 

### Step 2. Practice “One Inconvenient Situation” 
Your task this week is to complete one border protection action: 
* Refuse a request that is difficult or unpleasant for you to fulfill (colleague, friend, relative). 
* Make the refusal **short and without excuses**. For example: * “No, I won’t be able to help with this on Saturday. I have other plans”* (even if your plan is to just lie on the couch). 
* **Bear the guilt.** When you say no, a wave of guilt will rise within you. This is normal - the old neural track resists. Don't run to save the situation. Take a breath and exhale. The feeling of guilt will physiologically subside in 20 minutes, and the feeling of your own strength and preserved vegetative energy will remain with you.

## Manifesto of a new identity 

Write a 1-page document - this is not an affirmation or a dream. This is **engineering specification for the new you**. Format:

```
МАНИФЕСТ НОВОЙ ЛИЧНОСТИ

Date:___________ 

I, [name], declare the following: 

1. I am a person who [specific quality + specific action]. 
Example: “I am a person who does vestibular exercises every day 
and doesn’t miss a single day, even if he feels well.” 

2. I am NOT [specific old behavior] anymore. 
Example: “I no longer Google symptoms after 9:00 pm.” 

3. My boundaries: [3 specific rules]. 
Example: “I don’t take work calls after 7:00 p.m. I refuse 
meetings that don't bring me joy. I sleep at least 7 hours." 

4. My superpower, born from PPPD: [what did you get]. 
Example: “I learned to hear my body. I became more aware than 90% 
people around. I know my triggers and I know how to work with them.” 

5. My vow: [one sentence]. 
Example: “I will never go back to living on autopilot.” 

Signature:___________ 
``` 

Fill it out. Hang it on the wall. Re-read once a week. 

## Wheel of Balance 

It is a visual tool for assessing the current state of life. Draw a circle and divide it into 8 sectors: 

| Sector | Score (1–10) | What needs to change | 
| :--- | :---: | :--- | 
| **Health/Body** | __ | | 
| **Career/Job** | __ | | 
| **Finance** | __ | | 
| **Relationships/Family** | __ | | 
| **Friends/Society** | __ | | 
| **Personal growth** | __ | | 
| **Hobbies/Pleasures** | __ | | 
| **Spirituality/Meaning** | __ | | 

Give an honest rating from 1 to 10 in each sector. Connect the dots. The resulting figure is your “wheel”. If it looks like an even circle, you are in balance. If it’s on a star, it means that some spheres are sagging and pulling the rest along with them. 

**Rule:** PPPD almost always lives in “sagging” sectors. If “Health” is a 3 and “Work” is a 9, you’re on fire. If “Relationships” is 2, it means that unspoken grievances are accumulating in the muscles. 

## Boundaries by spheres 

For each “sagging” sector of the Wheel of Balance, set **one specific boundary**: 

* **Work:** *“I do not respond to management messages after 19:00”*. 
* **Relationships:** *“I tell my partner when I need time alone instead of silently getting angry.”* 
* **Society:** *“I refuse meetings that do not bring me joy, without explanation”*. 
* **Health:** *“I go to bed before 23:00, even if I haven’t finished watching the film”*. 
* **Finance:** *“I stop spending money on more “miracle drugs” and “healers””*. 

Boundaries are not selfishness. This is infrastructure. Without it, any system will fall sooner or later.
