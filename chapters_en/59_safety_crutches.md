# Safety Crutches: Subtle Avoidance Stealing Your Final 20% of Recovery

*You already leave the house, shop in supermarkets, and ride the subway. Yet an “emergency pill” still sits in your pocket just in case, your hand clutches a water bottle, and your eyes habitually scan for the nearest bench. Here is why these invisible crutches prevent your brain from shutting off dizziness completely.*

---

## 1. Stuck at 80%: “I Do Everything Right, Why Does the Background Swaying Remain?”

In my consultations, I constantly encounter people trapped in the exact same phase. Let us call it **the stubborn tail-end of PPPD**.

The person tells me:
> *“Maxim, I’ve read the foundational chapters, I do my vestibular drills, I walk 8,000 steps a day, I commute to work, and I go into shopping malls. The acute panic attacks are gone. Yet I still have a constant 2-out-of-10 background rocking sensation! Why haven’t the final 20% of my symptoms cleared up for three months?!”*

I start asking simple, surgical questions:
* *“Be honest with me: when you walk into a shopping mall, what is sitting in your right jacket pocket or purse?”* — *“Well... half a tablet of Xanax / Atarax. I haven’t taken one in two months! I just carry it ‘just in case’—it makes me feel safer.”*
* *“And when you walk down a busy avenue, what are your hands doing?”* — *“One hand is in my pocket gripping my phone, or I’m holding a bottle of ice water so I can take a sip if I feel floaty.”*
* *“And how do you stand in the checkout line?”* — *“I lean my hip against the counter or grip the shopping cart handle with both hands.”*

There is the answer. The person believes they are practicing exposure therapy. In reality, they are moving through the world wrapped in an invisible suit of **Safety Behaviors**.

## 2. The Neurobiology of the Crutch: Paul Salkovskis’ Discovery

In Cognitive Behavioral Therapy, this exact mechanism was identified and clinically proven by **Professor Paul Salkovskis** at the University of Oxford.

His finding is simple and unforgiving: **safety behaviors completely block inhibitory learning in the amygdala**.

Let us examine how your amygdala (your threat detection center) interprets a trip to the supermarket when you carry a “crutch” in your pocket:

> **What You Think (Prefrontal Cortex):**
> *“I went to the grocery store with my emergency pill in my pocket and held onto the shopping cart, and I didn’t faint — great job, I’m training my brain!”*
>
> **What Your Amygdala Records (Limbic System):**
> *“Alert! Today we entered a lethally dangerous supermarket! We narrowly escaped fainting and catastrophe ONLY BECAUSE we gripped the shopping cart with both hands, sipped cold water, and had an emergency sedative ready in our pocket! Conclusion: The supermarket is still deadly! Keep the vestibular alarm active!”*

Do you see the brilliance and cruelty of this trap?

Every single “precaution” you take serves as **direct proof to your survival brain that the threat is real**. Why would a firefighter take off their helmet and fireproof suit if they believe the building is still burning? As long as you carry a “fire extinguisher” in the form of an emergency pill or water bottle, your brain remains 100% convinced that the fire is still raging.

| Exposure Metric | Pseudo-Exposure (With Crutches) | Pure Exposure (Zero Crutches) |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Behavior** | Entering a mall wearing sunglasses, gripping a cart, carrying water & pills | Entering a mall empty-handed with unrestricted vision |
| **Attentional Focus** | Scanning for exits, benches, and calculating distance to home | Open presence in the environment |
| **Short-Term Effect** | Lower anxiety (4/10), illusion of control | Higher initial arousal for 10 minutes (7/10) |
| **Amygdala Appraisal** | “We survived only because of our safety gear! Threat is real!” | “We survived with zero protection! The alarm was false!” |
| **Long-Term Outcome** | Permanently plateaued at 70–80%, chronic low-grade swaying | Complete extinction of the fear reflex and 100% remission |

## 3. The Complete Registry of PPPD Safety Crutches

People dealing with chronic dizziness are remarkably resourceful. Over the years, I have cataloged 6 categories of hidden crutches that disguise themselves as “harmless habits”:

### 1. The Pharmacological Talisman (“The Pocket Pharmacy”)
A blister pack of benzodiazepines (Xanax, Klonopin, Ativan), hydroxyzine, beta-blockers, herbal drops, or strong menthol lozenges tucked into a wallet or bag. You may not have swallowed one in months, yet the moment you consider leaving it on the kitchen counter, panic flares up by the elevator: *“What if it hits me two miles from home and I have nothing on me?!”*

### 2. Tactile and Postural Anchors
* White-knuckling the handle of a grocery cart or stroller (using it as a medical walker).
* Automatically leaning a shoulder or hip against a wall, pillar, or handrail while waiting in line or riding the train.
* Walking exclusively along the edge of the sidewalk next to fences or building walls “just in case I need to grab something.”
* Stuffing hands deep into jacket pockets or crossing arms tightly across the chest (mechanically bracing the torso into a rigid splint).

### 3. Sensory Mufflers (Stimulus Shields)
* **Sunglasses or a low-brimmed cap** worn on overcast days or indoors inside supermarkets to cut off peripheral optical flow and fluorescent glare.
* **Noise-canceling headphones** worn outdoors or in stores to mute acoustic spatial volume.
* **Staring down at a smartphone screen** while walking or standing in line — you aren’t actually reading messages; you are using the phone as a fixed visual anchor to avoid looking at the open room.

### 4. Ritual Props
* **The Water Bottle:** Taking micro-sips of water every 3 minutes (because swallowing briefly stimulates the vagus nerve and interrupts sympathetic panic).
* **Pulse Oximeters & Smartwatches:** Checking heart rate on your wrist every 15 minutes (*“What’s my pulse right now? 96 bpm? Oh no, I need to sit down”*).

### 5. The Social Guide Dog (“I Don’t Sway When Someone Is With Me”)
Being willing to walk anywhere — provided you are holding your partner’s arm or walking alongside a friend. Or a subtler variation: walking alone while **talking on the phone the entire way**, using the person on the line as a “live lifeline” in case you collapse.

### 6. Geographical Escape Geometry
* Plotting walking routes strictly past pharmacies, urgent care clinics, or subway stations.
* Parking the car right next to the store entrance so the “retreat sprint” is under 30 yards.
* Choosing seats in movie theaters, restaurants, or airplanes strictly on the aisle closest to the exit or restroom.

## 4. Why the Final 20% of Dizziness Clings to Your Crutches

Recall the fundamental law of neuroplasticity from Module 3: **a neural fear circuit extinguishes only when a “Prediction Error” occurs**.

Your amygdala predicts: *“If we stand in the middle of a bright, crowded hypermarket without a cart, without water, and without a pill in our pocket, we will lose control and collapse onto the floor.”*

As long as you push a cart and keep a sedative in your pocket, **that prediction is never tested**! The amygdala never receives disconfirming evidence.

To erase the final 20% of background unsteadiness, your brain must experience raw, unbuffered reality:
1. I am standing in the center of a bright, busy space.
2. My hands are empty, my shoulders are dropped.
3. My emergency pills are sitting in a drawer 10 miles away.
4. My phone is zipped inside my bag.
5. A wave of rocking hit me — I did absolutely nothing to protect myself — and **nothing catastrophic happened**.

Only in that exact moment does a powerful inhibitory signal fire from the ventromedial prefrontal cortex to the amygdala: *“The threat is zero. Stand down.”*

## 5. The “Burn the Boats” Protocol: Disarming in 14 Days

Dropping your crutches feels uncomfortable at first — during the first 48 hours without them, your anxiety and swaying may temporarily tick up by 20–30%. Expect this and welcome it: you are taking the training wheels off the bicycle.

Follow this structured 14-day disarmament schedule:

### Phase 1. Emptying the Pockets (Days 1–3)
Take every “emergency” pill, sedative, herbal drop, mint, and water bottle out of your pockets, purse, and car glovebox, and leave them at home.
* Walk out the door empty-handed.
* When your mind bargains (*“What if a massive panic attack hits?!”*), answer firmly: *“Then we will experience the adrenaline surge raw and process it on the spot. My tools are in my head, not in a blister pack.”*

### Phase 2. Releasing Postural Props (Days 4–7)
* In the supermarket, swap the wheeled shopping cart for a **handheld basket** (or carry items in your hands).
* When standing in a checkout line or subway car, stand **in the open center**, without leaning your hip against a counter or bracing your back against a wall. Let your ankles and cerebellum micro-adjust your balance naturally.
* Take your hands out of your pockets while walking — let your arms swing freely in a natural cross-crawl gait pattern (essential for vestibular calibration!).

### Phase 3. Removing Sensory Filters (Days 8–10)
* Take off sunglasses and hoods indoors and on cloudy days.
* Remove headphones when walking down busy streets.
* Keep your phone in your pocket while waiting in lines and let your gaze rest naturally on the horizon and surrounding space.

### Phase 4. Reclaiming Geometry (Days 11–14)
* Sit in the middle of the row at a theater or café, with your back to the exit.
* Walk straight across the center of open plazas rather than hugging building walls.
* Take off your smartwatch or disable heart-rate notifications.
