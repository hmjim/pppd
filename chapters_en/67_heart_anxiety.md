# Nervous Tachycardia: Why Your Heart Pounds, Ectopic Beats, and "Skipped" Heartbeats

*Heart rate 120. Heart pounding in your throat. Then — bam! — it skipped a beat. Froze. Then started hammering again. You call an ambulance. ECG — normal. Holter monitor — isolated ectopic beats (a normal variant). The cardiologist says: "Your heart is healthy." But you don't believe it. Because you FEEL it glitching.*

---

## "There's Definitely Something Wrong with My Heart"

You're not alone. Cardiophobia — fear about your heart — is one of the most common symptoms in anxiety disorders. And one of the most agonizing. Because the heart isn't an arm or a back. It's LIFE. If it stops — that's it. Game over. No "reboot."

And so you start listening to it. Every second. You put your hand on your chest — counting beats. You bought a pulse oximeter — checking your heart rate 20 times a day. You downloaded an ECG app to your watch — and now you have 47 screenshots of "suspicious" rhythms.

Stop. Let's sort this out.

---

## What Adrenaline Does to the Heart

When you're anxious, the amygdala hits the "Danger!" button. The adrenal glands dump adrenaline. Adrenaline is a mobilization hormone. Its job is to prepare the body to flee from a predator.

What it does to the heart:
- **Speeds up the rhythm** (tachycardia): heart rate 100–140 bpm. This isn't a malfunction — it's NORMAL for a stress response. A runner's heart rate during a race hits 180 — and nothing terrible happens.
- **Strengthens contractions**: the heart beats harder, and you start to FEEL it. Normally you don't notice your heartbeat — it's background noise. Under adrenaline it becomes noticeable. This isn't pathology — it's high-performance mode.
- **Can trigger ectopic beats**: premature contractions followed by a compensatory pause. You feel this as a "skip" or a "drop." According to cardiologists, up to 200 ectopic beats per day is a completely normal variant. Anxious people get more of them because adrenaline increases myocardial excitability.

All of this is **a healthy heart operating normally under load**. Just like a car engine revs louder when you floor the gas pedal. It's not breaking down — it's executing a command.

---

## The Adrenaline Loop of Heart Fear

The mechanism is identical to PPPD — only instead of dizziness, the warning light is flashing on the heart:

1. You notice your heartbeat (or an ectopic beat)
2. Thought: "Something's wrong! Heart attack? Arrhythmia?"
3. Adrenaline → heart speeds up even more
4. "See! Confirmed! Heart rate 130!"
5. More adrenaline → even faster
6. Panic → call an ambulance

The ambulance arrives, runs an ECG, says "everything's fine." You calm down. The adrenaline burns off. Heart rate 72. And you think: "Okay, it's passed now. But IN THAT MOMENT it was genuinely terrifying."

Sound familiar? It's the same loop as with unsteadiness. Just a different channel.

---

## Why the Cardiologist Is Right and You're Wrong

**Fact 1:** A healthy heart does not stop from tachycardia. A heart rate of 140 during a panic attack is the same as a heart rate of 140 while running. You're not afraid your heart will stop during a jog, are you?

**Fact 2:** Ectopic beats caused by anxiety are benign. They don't progress into fibrillation. They don't cause cardiac arrest. Cardiologists consider them clinically insignificant if the heart's structure is normal (on echocardiogram).

**Fact 3:** A heart attack is a coronary artery blocked by a blood clot. It is NOT caused by anxiety. A heart attack requires atherosclerosis, a clot, vascular damage. Anxiety doesn't create blood clots. Your heart is beating fast not because it's diseased — but because you revved it up with adrenaline.

---

## What to Do

### 1. Get Tested (Once)
ECG, echocardiogram (cardiac ultrasound), Holter monitor (24-hour monitoring). The cardiologist said "normal"? Done. Close that door. Don't repeat tests every month — that feeds the anxiety.

### 2. Ditch the Pulse Oximeter
Seriously. Throw it out. Or shove it in a closet. Every heart rate measurement is an act of monitoring that tells your brain: "The heart is under threat, stay vigilant." The more you check — the stronger the anxiety.

### 3. Apply the "5-Minute Rule"
Heart starts pounding? Say: "This is adrenaline. It will burn off in 5 minutes. My heart is healthy — the cardiologist confirmed it. I'm just observing." Don't lie down, don't grab the sedative drops, don't call your mom. Stand there and wait. In 5 minutes your heart rate will start to drop.

### 4. Stop Listening for It
Every time you put your hand on your chest or feel for a pulse on your neck — you're reinforcing the neural pathway "heart = danger." Remove your hand. Occupy your brain with something else. A pathway that isn't used grows over.

## Connection to PPPD

Tachycardia, ectopic beats, and cardiophobia are frequent companions of PPPD. Because the root cause is the same: **an overloaded nervous system** sending alarm signals through every channel. Today — unsteadiness. Tomorrow — the heart. The day after — a lump in the throat.

These aren't three different diseases. It's one overheated engine. And the fix is the same: CBT, exposure, working through anxiety and the root causes of stress. More details in the chapter [Psychosomatics: One Engine, Different Warning Lights](41_psychosomatics.html).
