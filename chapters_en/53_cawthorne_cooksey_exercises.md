# Cawthorne-Cooksey Exercises for PPPD: What to Do, How to Do It, and How Long to Wait

*Vestibular rehabilitation is the foundation of PPPD treatment. Not SSRIs, not therapy, not meditation. The exercises. Every single day. Without them — nothing works. Let's break down the Cawthorne-Cooksey protocol.*

## The Core Principle: Your Brain Only Learns from Errors

Burn this into your memory. It explains everything.

When you do a vestibular exercise and it feels **slightly uncomfortable** — your brain gets a signal: "Whoa, there's a mismatch between the eyes and the vestibular system. Time to recalibrate." And it starts rewiring neural connections. This is [neuroplasticity](14_neuroplasticity.html) in action.

If you do the exercises so slowly and carefully that you feel perfectly comfortable — your brain learns nothing. Why would it change when "everything's fine"?

If you go so hard that you're nauseous and reeling — your brain panics and shuts down. Overload. Defense mode.

The sweet spot: **discomfort at 3–4 out of 10**. A slight wobble, a gentle "things are drifting" sensation — but within 2–3 minutes after the exercise, everything settles. That's the ideal workload.

Think of the gym: if the barbell is too light — your muscles don't grow. Too heavy — you get injured. The right working weight is the one that's hard but doable.

## Stages of the Cawthorne-Cooksey Protocol

### Stage 1: Sitting (Weeks 1–2)

You're sitting in a chair. Back straight. Feet on the floor.

**Eyes:**
- Look up and down, then left and right. Start slowly, then speed up. 20 repetitions for each direction.
- Extend your finger at arm's length. Slowly bring it toward your nose (about 6 inches away) and back. Eyes track the finger. 20 reps.

**Head:**
- Tilt forward and back. Slowly, then faster. 20 reps.
- Turn left and right. 20 reps.
- First with eyes open, then — with eyes closed.

When you close your eyes, the brain loses its visual crutch and is forced to rely on the vestibular system. That's exactly what trains compensation.

### Stage 2: Standing (Weeks 2–4)

Everything the same, but standing. Gravity adds load.

Plus:
- **Toss a ball** from hand to hand at eye level. Eyes follow the ball. This trains coordination between eyes + hands + vestibular system.
- **Sit → stand → sit** — rise and sit down. 10 times with eyes open, then 10 with eyes closed. The brain gets used to position changes without panicking.

### Stage 3: Moving (Weeks 4–8)

This is where the real work begins:

- **Walking across the room with head turns.** Walk straight while turning your head left and right with each step. The brain has to maintain balance when vision and motion are out of sync.
- **Figure-eight walking** around two chairs. Constant turns, body tilts — the brain processes a complex stream of sensory data.
- **Romberg stance on a foam pad.** Stand upright, feet together, on a soft surface. Eyes closed. 30–60 seconds. This is maximum vestibular load — all three support systems are challenged (soft surface = weak proprioception, closed eyes = no vision, standing = vestibular system maxed out).

## How Long Should You Practice?

**15–20 minutes a day. Every day. No days off.**

This isn't a gym workout where you need rest days. The brain is constantly rewiring — and it needs daily stimulus. Miss a day — you slide back half a step. Miss a week — you slide back two.

Like brushing your teeth. Not up for debate. You just do it.

## When Will It Get Better?

Honest timelines:

- **Weeks 1–2:** It might get worse. That's normal. The brain has started rewiring — and it's uncomfortable with the process. Don't quit. It's like muscle soreness after your first workout — a sign that something is actually working.
- **Weeks 4–6:** First improvements. Visual dependence decreases — the grocery store becomes more bearable. [Brain fog](33_brain_fog.html) starts to lift.
- **Months 3–6:** Stable results. Confident gait, unsteadiness fades into the background and no longer scares you. The brain processes signals automatically — you don't need to "think" about balance.

## Common Mistakes

**"I do it when I'm in the mood"** — no. Every day. Not in the mood — you do it without the mood.

**"I go super slowly so I don't get dizzy"** — no. Mild dizziness during the exercise is the goal, not a side effect.

**"It's been 2 weeks, nothing's changed, I quit"** — no. Two weeks is just the warm-up. Neuroplasticity requires a minimum of 4–6 weeks for the first lasting changes.

**"The exercises made me worse, so they're not for me"** — no. Temporary worsening in the first days is sensory recalibration. The brain is rebooting. If the worsening lasts more than 2 hours after a session — dial down the intensity, but don't stop.
