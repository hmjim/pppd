# Brain fog: causes, heaviness in the head and connection with dizziness 

*“Head feels like cotton wool”, “difficult to think”, “like in an aquarium” - an analysis of the causes of psychogenic fog in the head.*

---

## What is Brain Fog? 

**Brain fog** is not a medical diagnosis, but a common set of symptoms in which a person experiences: 
- Slowness of thought processes 
- Feeling of a “heavy” or “cast iron” head 
- Problems with concentration and memory 
- Feeling of unreality of what is happening (derealization) 
- Associated feeling of slight unsteadiness or swaying 

Patients often turn to neurologists with suspicion of “poor blood supply to the brain” or “vascular problems,” but in most cases the reasons lie in functional fatigue of the central nervous system and increased anxiety. 

## Causes of brain fog 

### 1. PPPD (persistent postural-perceptual dizziness) 
In PPPD, the brain will constantly spend up to 80% of its processing resources on manual control of balance. As a result, there is simply “not enough RAM” for the usual cognitive tasks (memory, attention, analysis). 

### 2. Chronic stress and anxiety 
Constantly elevated levels of cortisol and adrenaline keep the nervous system in a state of exhaustion. The brain goes into energy-saving mode. 

### 3. Derealization and depersonalization 
With severe anxiety, the protective “fuse” of the psyche turns on: perception becomes muffled, as if through cloudy glass. 

### 4. Muscle tension in the neck and scalp 
Chronic tension in the collar area leads to a feeling of heaviness, a “helmet” on the head and dull discomfort. 

## Myths about brain fog 

- ❌ **“This is a disturbance in the blood flow of the brain”** - Ultrasound scanning of the neck vessels with fog in the head almost always shows normal. 
- ❌ **“It’s from osteochondrosis”** - Osteochondrosis does not cause cognitive fog. 
- ❌ **“You need to take nootropics”** - Nootropics do not eliminate the functional overload of the brain from anxiety. 

## How to remove brain fog 

1. **Reduce over-control over balance.** Start performing vestibular gymnastics and reduce control over steps. 
2. **Relieve body tension.** Use [progressive relaxation](05_relaxation.html) and work the neck muscles. 
3. **Normalize sleep.** Read detailed recommendations for restoring sleep during dizziness in the specialized sections of the book. 
4. **Go through differential diagnosis.** Make sure other causes are excluded (ferritin/B12 deficiencies, hypothyroidism).
