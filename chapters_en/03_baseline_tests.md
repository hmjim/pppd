# Digitization: where are you now

*You can't fix something you haven't measured. Before you start, fix your starting point.*

---

## Why is this needed

You will work on yourself for months. And at some point it will seem to you that nothing is changing. That the dizziness is the same as it was. That anxiety doesn't go away. That everything is meaningless.

This is deception. The brain gets used to the current state and forgets what it was like before. You can go halfway and not even notice it.

Therefore, we will now make a **measuring point**. You fill out two short tests, write down the results and put them in a drawer. In a month, two, three, you’ll go through it again. And you will see the difference in numbers, not in sensations. Feelings lie. Numbers - no.

## Test 1: HADS - Anxiety and Depression

HADS (Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale) is a standardized questionnaire for assessing anxiety and depression. It is used all over the world. 14 questions, takes 5 minutes.

It is divided into two scales:
- **Anxiety Scale (HADS-A)** - 7 questions.
- **Depression Scale (HADS-D)** - 7 questions.

Each scale has a maximum of 21 points.

| Points | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- |
| 0–7 | Norma |
| 8–10 | Subclinical anxiety/depression (borderline) |
| 11–21 | Clinically Significant Anxiety/Depression |

Most people with PPPD fall in the 12–18 range for anxiety. This is normal for this condition. Don't be scared by the numbers - you need them to track your progress.

### How to interpret your HADS

Numbers are not a sentence. These are the coordinates on the map. Here's what they mean practically:

**Anxiety 15-21 (severe):** Your amygdala is firing on all cylinders. You most likely wake up anxious, unable to relax even in a safe environment, and perceive any physical symptom as a threat to your life. At this level, CBT techniques (logical analysis of thoughts) will work poorly - the brain is too overheated for logic. Start with the body: Jacobson relaxation, contrast shower, walking. When anxiety drops to 10–12, use cognitive tools.

**Anxiety 8–14 (moderate):** You are functioning, but with difficulty. There is background tension, periodic bursts of panic, but in between there are “windows” of clarity. This is a work area - the exposition, the SERM diary, and metacognitive techniques are great here.

**Alarm 0–7 (normal):** If you are already here, you are almost at the finish line. Residual unsteadiness at this level is most likely a muscle pattern rather than an active neurosis. Work with your body and don’t forget about practice hygiene.

**Depression 11+ (significant):** This is an important marker. Depression in PPPD is often secondary - you are not born depressed, you are tired of the endless struggle. But if the indicator is consistently above 14, discuss with your doctor the possibility of drug support (SSRIs). This is not a weakness, it is a resource to have enough strength to practice.

You can find the test by googling “HADS test online.” Choose any option - they are all the same.

## Test 2: DHI - dizziness index

DHI (Dizziness Handicap Inventory) is an international standard for assessing how dizziness interferes with your life. 25 questions, 5 minutes.

Each question: “Yes” (4 points), “Sometimes” (2 points), “No” (0 points). Maximum - 100 points.

| Points | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- |
| 0–30 | Easy degree |
| 31–60 | Moderate |
| 61–100 | Severe |

### What does your DHI mean in practice?

**DHI 61–100:** Dizziness controls your life. You probably don't go to stores, avoid transport, and are afraid to be alone. It's hard, but this is the most rewarding area to work in: when you start practicing exposure, each point will drop significantly. People with a DHI of 80 often drop to 40–50 within 2 months.

**DHI 31–60:** You are functioning, but with limitations. You go to the store - but with difficulty. You go to work - but with discomfort. It is in this zone that people most often get “stuck” because they “don’t feel bad enough to change anything.” Don't get stuck. Keep working.

**DHI 0–30:** Dizziness is present, but does not interfere with life. You're at the finish line. Residual symptoms at this level are normal system noise and not disease.

**Clinically significant improvement** is a decrease in DHI by **18 points or more**. If you were 72 and became 50, it’s not “almost nothing has changed.” This is mathematically proven progress that your frightened brain is trying to discount.

Also searched for “DHI test dizziness”.

## Additionally: Holmes-Rahe scale

If you want to understand *why* you ended up here in the first place, take the **Holmes-Rahe Stressful Events Scale**. It is not about the current state, but about the accumulated stress over the past 12 months.

Each life event (divorce, job loss, moving, birth of a child, illness of a loved one) is assigned points. You add it up and you get the picture.

| Points | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- |
| Less than 150 | Low risk of stress-related illness |
| 150–299 | Moderate risk |
| 300+ | High risk |

Many people, after taking this test, realize for the first time: “Wow, I scored 380 points. No wonder it's creeping me out." And this specific figure is already therapy. Because she explains what is happening not with mysticism, but with mathematics.

## How to write

Take a piece of paper. Or a note on your phone. Write down today's date and results:

```
Дата: ___________

HADS-A (alarm): ___ / 21
HADS-D (depression): ___ / 21
DHI (dizziness): ___ / 100
Holmes-Rahe (stress): ___ points
```

That's it. This is your point A. We'll go from here.
