## The Habituation Trap: Why Feelings Lie and Numbers Don't

Recovering from PPPD takes weeks and months, not three days. Almost everyone hits a wall around month two or three where it feels like: *"Nothing is changing. I'm still swaying. None of this is working."*

This is a classic neurocognitive illusion called **baseline shifting**. Your brain rapidly adapts to each new level of function and forgets how restricted your life was four weeks ago. Two months ago you may have been afraid to walk outside alone; today you spent thirty minutes under bright supermarket lights and felt mild rocking at the checkout — yet an anxious brain labels today a "failure" and erases massive progress.

That is why we rely on **hard clinical metrics**, never daily mood:
1. Once every 30 days, complete the three standardized scales in the **[PPPD Online Diagnostic Suite](52_dhi_hads_niigata_tests.html)** (**DHI**, **HADS**, and **Niigata NPQ**).
2. Log your scores in the **90-Day Progress Tracker** and audit your 12-month cumulative stress load with the **Holmes–Rahe Scale** below.

## Tactical Key to Your Scores: Where to Start

Scores are coordinates on an engineering map, not a life sentence. They tell you **which tool to deploy first** so you don't waste energy:

| Your Metric | What Is Happening Neurologically | Optimal Starting Strategy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Anxiety HADS-A 15–21** | The amygdala is red-lining and overriding the prefrontal cortex. Every bodily sensation registers as mortal danger. | Pure cognitive debate works poorly when the brain is overheated. Start **bottom-up through the body**: Jacobson PMR, daily walks, sleep stabilization. Add CBT once HADS-A drops to 10–12. |
| **Anxiety HADS-A 8–14** | Moderate background tension with periodic windows of clarity. | Prime working zone. Combine daily VRT gaze drills, dropping safety crutches, and structured graded exposure. |
| **Burnout HADS-D > 14** | Secondary nervous system exhaustion from months of fighting chronic dizziness. | Not a character flaw. If HADS-D stays above 14, discuss temporary SSRI/SNRI neuromodulation with your physician to restore bandwidth for rehab. |
| **Handicap DHI 61–100** | Life has shrunk around avoidance of stores, transit, and walking alone. | Highest-yield zone: once systematic exposure begins, DHI scores of 75–85 often drop to 40–45 within the first 8 weeks. |
| **Handicap DHI 31–60** | The dangerous **"tolerable plateau"** — functioning with constant effort and subtle avoidance. | Most people quit rehab here because life feels "survivable." Push all the way through and eliminate subtle balance checking. |
| **DHI Drop of ≥ 18 pts** | International **Minimal Clinically Important Difference (MCID)**. | Dropping from 72 to 52 is mathematically verified neural recalibration — even if your anxious brain tries to dismiss it. |

## The Holmes–Rahe Stress Scale: Why Your System Overloaded

PPPD rarely appears out of nowhere. Even when triggered by acute BPPV, vestibular neuritis, or a panic attack, your autonomic nervous system was almost always running on empty beforehand. The **Holmes–Rahe Life Stress Inventory** measures **cumulative autonomic load over the past 12 months** — including positive major transitions (moving, marriage, promotion, new child) that still demand heavy adaptation:

| 12-Month Score | Autonomic Nervous System Load |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Under 150 points** | Low cumulative load — normal physiological reserve |
| **150–299 points** | Moderate overload — ~50% risk of stress-induced somatic dysregulation |
| **300+ points** | High overload (~80% somatization risk) — your nervous system was running past redline |

Seeing a concrete number like **340 points** is therapeutic in itself: it replaces catastrophic mystery (*"something inexplicable broke inside my brain"*) with clear cause-and-effect biology.
