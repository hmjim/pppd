# Exercise machines: how to give your brain the right load

*Why gymnastics alone is not enough - and what to do next when the basic exercises have already been mastered.*

---

## 1. The essence and problems: the brain does not trust its sensors

In the previous chapter, we started the process of calibrating the vestibular apparatus. Basic exercises are the foundation. But with PPPD, there is another layer of the problem that Kavtorn-Kuksi gymnastics does not completely cover.

The brain with PPPD is in two interconnected states:

1. **Sensory conflict** - vision is overloaded with trust, vestibular and proprioception are ignored. The brain refuses to redistribute weight between channels.
2. **Sympathetic hyperexcitability** - the nervous system is constantly “on edge”. In this state, neuroplasticity is weakened: the brain does not learn, it survives.

Standard vestibular gymnastics trains the first. This section is about how to solve the latter, and how to enhance the former through more specific techniques.

## 2. Physiological explanation: three channels and one distrustful editor

Three systems maintain balance:
- **Vestibular** - acceleration sensor in the ear
- **Visual** - picture of the outside world
- **Proprioceptive** - pressure, tension, body position in space

Normally, the brain constantly weighs all three signals. With PPPD, a recalibration occurred: vision received a disproportionately large weight, and the other two channels were frozen. The brain has become “visually dependent.”

Result: any moving visual field becomes destabilizing. Supermarket, crowd, escalator, scrolling - the brain interprets this as a threat, produces motion and anxiety. The circle is closed.

> **Point of Support:** The brain is not broken. He retrained in the wrong direction. It can be retrained back. It will take time, but the mechanism works.

---

## 3. When NOT to do these exercises

Before you start, check the list. This is not insurance, this is elementary logic: you cannot train a system that is currently overloaded.

**Hard stop:**
- You may have BPPV (benign paroxysmal positional vertigo) - first Epley/Semont maneuvers, then VOR
- Acute phase of PPPD - the first 1-2 weeks after the onset of symptoms
- Any acute illness: ARVI, high fever, severe lack of sleep (less than 5 hours last night)
- Yesterday there was a strong pullback - take a break for 1 day

**Situational stop:**
- Anxiety right now is 8+/10 - first HRV breathing to 5/10, then training
- You just ate - wait 40–60 minutes
- My head is “exploding” at 9/10 right now - this is not a “good load”, this is an overload

## 4. Practice: Four Techniques for Advanced Recalibration

### Technique 1: VOR Training - Stable Gaze with Head Movement

**How is it different from regular gymnastics:**
These are not just “nods”. This forces the vestibulo-ocular reflex (VOR), a reflex that keeps the gaze stable when the head moves. In PPPD it is deregulated.

**How to do:**

*Basic level (weeks 1–2):*
1. Choose a point on the wall at eye level - a letter, a cross, a corner of a frame.
2. Fix your gaze firmly. Don't tear it off.
3. Move your head left and right - the point should remain **clear**.
4. Blurred - slow down. Clarity = criterion for correct tempo.
5. Start with 20-30 movements per minute, 1-2 minutes, pause 30 seconds.

*Intermediate (weeks 3–4):*
- The same movements while standing.
- Add a vertical plane (nod up and down).
- Tempo up to 60–80 movements per minute.

*Advanced level (weeks 5+):*
- Walking with simultaneous turns of the head left and right.
- Look at the horizon, not at your feet.

---

### Technique 2: Optokinetic training - habituation to a moving background

**The essence of the problem:**
A crowd, a supermarket, a flickering screen, an escalator—all these are “optokinetic stimuli.” With PPPD, the brain reacts to them as a threat: it produces nausea, unsteadiness, and anxiety. This is **not weakness**. This is a trained reflex.

**How to do:**

You need visual movement - a moving pattern, streaming video, a moving stream of objects.

- Look **relaxed**, without trying to follow individual objects
- Don’t “hold on” with your gaze - let the background move
- Start with 30 seconds, add 15–20 seconds every 2–3 days
- Discomfort 3–4/10 – work area ✓
- Discomfort 7+/10 - stop, too early

*Progression by level:*

| Level | Source of stimulation | Difficulty |
|---------|----|-----------|
| 1 | Stripes/dots moving slowly (horizontal) | Low |
| 2 | Fast moving pattern | Average |
| 3 | First person video (movement through space) | High |
| 4 | Real environments: escalators, crowds, transport | Exposure |

---

### Technique 3: Saccades - restoration of rapid gaze switching

**The essence of the problem:**
Saccades are quick, precise jumps of gaze from object to object. With PPPD, the oculomotor system is overloaded: the brain is accustomed to holding its gaze motionless, afraid of losing its visual “fulcrum.” Switching gaze between objects causes a short-term loss of landmarks. Saccade training restores confident and rapid switching without disorientation.

**How to do:**

1. Hang two objects on the wall at a distance of 40–60 cm from each other (letters, numbers).
2. Move your gaze from one to another - **quickly and accurately**, without “floating” between them.
3. The head is **stationary** - only the eyes work.
4. 20–30 translations, pause, repeat.

*Complication:*
- Three or more points in a chaotic order - name the object out loud before looking at it.
- Add head movement simultaneously with saccades.

---

### Technique 4: HRV breathing - transferring the NS to training mode

**Why is this necessary:**
Neuroplasticity—the brain's ability to change neural connections—is significantly suppressed when cortisol levels are chronically high. When the nervous system is constantly on alert, the brain saves resources for survival rather than for learning. If you train the vestibular system in a state of high anxiety, the effect is minimal.

HRV (heart rate variability) breathing is a technique that directly activates the vagus nerve and switches the NS to parasympathetic.

**Resonance frequency: 5-6 breaths per minute.**

*Protocol:*
1. Inhale through the nose - 4 seconds
2. Exhale through the mouth - 6 seconds
3. 10 cycles = ~1.5 minutes

Do this **before** every VOR or optokinetics workout. This is not meditation or affirmations. This is a direct physiological switching of the nervous system into a mode in which the brain is able to retrain.

## 6. What progress looks like - improvement markers

People quit after 2 weeks because they don’t know what to consider as an improvement. Here are concrete signals that the system is working - even if it seems that “nothing is changing.”

**Weeks 1-3: Microsignals**
- Optokinetics for 30 seconds gave 8/10 → now 4/10 with the same duration
- After VOR, the head “comes to its senses” in 5 minutes instead of 20
- I began to look less at my feet on the street - I noticed this after the fact

**Weeks 4-6: noticeable changes**
- You can spend 5 minutes longer in the supermarket without increasing anxiety
- Saccades have become more accurate - objects “do not float”
- In the morning, when you open your eyes, the motion goes faster

**Weeks 7-12: Functional Wins**
- Riding in a vehicle with objects moving outside the window is no longer a disaster
- You can turn your head in a conversation without thinking
- Scrolling the phone feed is no longer a trigger

> If after 4 weeks none of the microsignals have appeared, check: are you doing exercises in sympathy (anxiety above 6)? This is the main reason for zero progress.

## 7. Dosage rules

**80% Rule:** Do 80% of your limit. Mild discomfort 3–4/10 is normal. Severe dizziness for more than 30 minutes after - too much, reduce the volume.

**Rule of regularity:** 15 minutes every day is better than an hour once a week. The brain learns through frequent repetition, not through one-time intensity.

**Parasympathetic rule:** if anxiety is now above 6/10 - first HRV breathing, and only then training. In high anxiety states, exercise has minimal effect and can provide negative reinforcement.

---

## 8. Morning ritual: 7 minutes that set the tone for the day

Instead of training randomly, build it into the morning. The optimal time is 45–60 minutes after waking up: the peak of the cortisol response (Cortisol Awakening Response) occurs in the first 20–30 minutes after waking up, after which the level begins to subside and the brain enters a more stable operating state.

**Sequence (7 minutes total):**
1. **Stand up → 3 minutes of HRV breathing** (inhale 4 sec, exhale 6 sec, sitting on a bed or chair)
2. **2 minutes VOR** (sitting, horizontal, point on the wall opposite)
3. **1 minute of saccades** (two objects on the wall)
4. **1 minute optokinetics** (moving pattern when you reach the desired level)

This is not a replacement for a full workout - it is a daily “drip” for the vestibular system. The sequence is important: first parasympathetic, then load.

## 9. Frequently asked questions

**"It got worse after the first workout - did I break something?"**
No. A temporary increase in symptoms in the first 2-3 days is a normal reaction to an unusual load. The brain has received a new signal that it does not know how to process - while it is learning, symptoms may temporarily increase. Post-session discomfort that resolves within 15–30 minutes is normal. If more than an hour does not pass, reduce the volume, do not stop completely.

**"How long will it take for the results to appear?"**
The first microsignals occur in 2–3 weeks. Noticeable changes occur within 4–6 weeks. Sustainable functional result – 3–6 months. This is not a course - it is brain retraining. The brain is slow but reliable.

**“Can I do all four techniques at once?”**
No. One new technique every 2 weeks. Overloading the vestibular system is not “working hard”, it is negative reinforcement. The brain will remember the workout as a stressor rather than as a safe experience.

**“I’m fine when I’m lying down and bad when I get up - is it the same story?”**
Most likely yes. In a vertical position, the vestibular system works at full capacity, plus visual flow is added. If there are no symptoms while lying down, this is a good sign: start training while sitting, move on to standing exercises only after 2 weeks of confident progress. If there are strong symptoms while lying down, you need to rule out other causes by a doctor.

## 10. What these techniques DO NOT do

The physical part is only half of the recovery. These techniques:
- ✓ Reduce sensory conflict
- ✓ Restores VOR and optokinetic tolerance
- ✓ Switch the NS to parasympathetic
- ✗ **Does not eliminate fear** - this is the work of exposure and CBT
- ✗ **Do not remove the belief “there is something wrong with me”** - this is the work of cognitive restructuring
- ✗ **Do not work at full capacity** without parallel psychological work
