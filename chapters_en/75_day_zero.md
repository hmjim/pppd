# Day Zero: What to Do If You've Just Been Told "PPPD"

*You just found out. Maybe a neurologist gave you the diagnosis. Maybe you found this word on the internet and realized — that's you. Right now your head is full of chaos, panic, and dozens of questions. This chapter is your first aid. Not for tomorrow. For right now.*

---

## Breathe Out. You're Not Dying.

First and foremost: PPPD is **not life-threatening**. Not a single person in history has died from PPPD. Not one has fainted from it. Not one had a burst blood vessel. No stroke, no heart attack, no tumor.

PPPD is a software glitch in the nervous system. Your "hardware" (brain, blood vessels, inner ear) — is fine. The glitch is in the "software": the brain is incorrectly processing signals from the vestibular system. Like a computer with too many tabs open — it's lagging, but it's not broken.

And this bug **can be fixed**. Completely. Not "learn to live with it" — but get back to zero. The author of this book walked this path from "can't make it to the grocery store" to "flying across the world without a single symptom." And you will too.

---

## Three Things You Need to Do TODAY

### 1. Close Google

Seriously. Close all tabs with forums, articles, illness stories. Every time you read "I've had PPPD for 5 years and nothing has helped me" — you're feeding your anxiety. And anxiety feeds your symptoms. The adrenaline loop: fear → adrenaline → unsteadiness → even more fear.

You found this book — that means you have a guide. The rest of the internet is not what you need right now. It's poison.

### 2. Stop Evaluating Your Condition

Right now there's a counter running in your head: "Am I swaying? More or less than yesterday? Is this a new symptom?" This internal scanner is the main engine of PPPD. It's called the CAS trap (Cognitive Attentional Syndrome), and it has its own dedicated chapter.

For today — just notice that you're doing it. Every time you catch yourself scanning your body, say: "I noticed. This is CAS. I'm not going to evaluate." Don't fight the thoughts — just tag them and switch your attention.

### 3. Breathe

Not "take deep breaths" — a specific technique. The physiological sigh: double inhale through the nose (80% + 20%) → maximum-length exhale through the mouth (8–10 seconds). Three times. Takes 30 seconds. Activates the vagus nerve and switches the nervous system from "fight-or-flight" mode to "recovery" mode. Works in a single cycle.

---

## What NOT to Do

- **Don't lie down.** The more you lie down — the more you'll sway when you get up. The brain needs vestibular input, not silence.
- **Don't call friends and your mom to complain.** Every retelling of symptoms is rumination that reinforces the neural pathway of fear. If you need support — say: "I feel bad, but I'm figuring it out. I don't need advice."
- **Don't buy Mexidol, Actovegin, Cavinton.** These drugs don't work for PPPD. Don't waste your money.
- **Don't go for an MRI for the third time.** If tests have already been done and everything is normal — additional checks only feed health anxiety.
