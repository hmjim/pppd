# PPPD and dizziness - why do you feel dizzy all the time? 

*We’ll figure out why people with PPPD feel constantly dizzy despite the normal results of all examinations. Mechanism, types of dizziness and what to do about it.* 

## Dizziness with PPPD is not what you think 

When a person says “I’m dizzy,” they usually imagine a carousel - everything is spinning, the walls are moving, you can’t stand up. This is **true rotational vertigo** (vertigo). This is usually not the case with PPPD. 

Dizziness with PPPD is different: 
- **Swaying** - like on a ship in a calm 
- **Swimming** - “the earth is like liquid” 
- **Unsteadiness** - the world slightly “shakes” or “floats” 
- **Instability** - “the legs are not mine”, “the floor is moving away” 

This is **non-systemic dizziness** - no sensation of rotation, no nystagmus, no falling. But it feels no less painful. 

## Why do you feel dizzy with PPPD? 

Your brain receives information about body position from three systems: 

1. **Eyes** - see where is up and down 
2. **Vestibular apparatus** (inner ear) - senses tilts and turns 
3. **Proprioception** - muscles and joints tell you where each part of the body is located 

Normally, the brain automatically compares data from all three. In PPPD, this process is disrupted - not because “the sensors are broken”, but because **the brain is stuck in a hypervigilance mode**. 

Chronic stress → cortisol release → [muscle armor](04_muscle_armor.html) → neck tightening → distortion of proprioceptive signals → desynchronization of three systems → feeling of dizziness. 

## How does PPPD differ from other types of dizziness? 

| Sign | PPPD | BPPV | Meniere's disease | Vestibular neuronitis | 
|---------|------|------|-----------------|----------------------| 
| **Type** | Non-systemic (swaying) | Rotational | Rotational | Rotational | 
| **Duration of attack** | Hours–days (background) | 5–60 seconds | 20 min–12 hrs | Days–weeks | 
| **Trigger** | Stress, visual load | Head turn | Spontaneously | Spontaneously | 
| **Rumour** | Norma | Norma | Reduced | Norma | 
| **MRI** | Norma | Norma | Norma | Norma | 
| **Treatment** | CBT + gymnastics | Epley Maneuver | Medicines | Gymnastics + time | 

**Important:** PPPV often develops **after** BPPV or vestibular neuronitis. The attack itself has passed, but the brain “remembered” the feeling and continues to reproduce it. This is [adrenaline loop](09_adrenaline_loop.html). 

## When the dizziness gets worse

Typical provocateurs for PPPD: 
- **Vertical position** - standing and walking is worse, lying down is easier 
- **Morning** - after sleep the brain does not have time to “recalibrate” the balance 
- **Shops, shopping centers, metro** - [visual addiction](08_visual_dependence.html) overloads the brain 
- **Stress and anxiety** - any emotional stress increases unsteadiness 
- **Fatigue and lack of sleep** - [sleep and PPPD](26_sleep.html) are closely related 
- **Phone and screens** - scrolling causes visual dizziness 

## How to treat dizziness with PPPD 

PPPD is a **completely reversible condition**. A detailed treatment system is described in the book “[Point of Support](../)”: 

1. [Vestibular gymnastics](06_vestibular.html) - “retraining” the brain to process balance signals 
2. [Exposure](12_exposure.html) - gradual return to frightening situations 
3. [Relaxation](05_relaxation.html) - relieving muscle tension that feeds dizziness 
4. Working with anxiety - [metacognitive therapy](15_metacognition.html), [cognitive distortions](16_cognitive_distortions.html) 
5. Physical activity - [sport](13_sport.html) reduces cortisol and stimulates neuroplasticity
