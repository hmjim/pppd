# Ego: dismantling the false personality brick by brick 

*You think that you are your name, profession, status and set of habits. What if it’s not you?*

---

## Advanced level 

If you have reached this chapter, you have already completed the base. You know what PPPD is. You are working with the body. You've broken the adrenaline loop. You're doing CBT. 

Now we go deeper. 

There is a moment “here and now”. The past no longer exists - it has passed. The future does not exist yet - it has not arrived. There is only this moment. And in this moment I will ask you several questions that can knock down the door in your mind. 

Who does your cognitive dissonance come to? 

To whom does anxiety come? 

Who gets dizziness? 

**Who are you anyway?** 

## I am not “me” 

Not me as a name. Not me as a programmer, manager, mother, accountant. Not me as a “good person” or a “bad person.” Not me as a “family man” or a “loner.” 

All these labels are not you. It's **ego**. 

The ego is a construct. A set of bricks that other people gave you. My parents gave me some building blocks: “you must be obedient,” “boys don’t cry,” “good girls don’t get angry.” Some were given in kindergarten, school, army, at work. Some - the street, the Internet, advertising. 

You didn't choose these bricks. They were **invested** for you. 

## Coca-Cola advertising 

Maybe a dumb example, but it explains it well. 

Do you remember the Coca-Cola commercials? New Year, red trucks, Santa Claus, “The holiday is coming to us.” Shown for years, since childhood. And now you have a can of Coca-Cola on your New Year’s table. 

Congratulations. It's not yours. This was forced on you. 

You didn't want a Coke. You wanted tea with lemon. Or he didn’t want anything at all. But smart guys in advertising agencies have been putting the association in your head for decades: “New Year = Coca-Cola = happiness.” 

Now scale that to life. 

## Imposed paradigms of happiness 

Apartment, car, family, career. Helicopter, yacht, penthouse - for those who have “achieved more.” This is the paradigm of life that was invented for us. As per Maslow’s table - satisfy your needs, climb the pyramid, and you will be happy. 

Think about it. Is this really true? Is a car really a measure of happiness? 

If you talk to people who have a car, an apartment, and a family, many of them are more unhappy than you. Because they were chasing someone else's template, achieved it - and discovered emptiness. And emptiness is anxiety. And anxiety is hello, PPPD. 

## What does dizziness have to do with it? 

And here's what it has to do with it. 

When you don’t live your own life - when you follow other people’s attitudes for years, suppress your desires, tolerate what you don’t need to tolerate - your body begins to scream. Because the body doesn't know how to lie.

You can deceive your head: “Everything is fine, I like my job, my marriage, my city.” But the body hears something else. And it sends a signal - first quiet (fatigue, tension), then louder (headaches, cramps), and then screams at everyone (panic, dizziness, PPPD). 

**Neurosis is the body’s cry when you betray yourself.** 

## Knock out other people's bricks 

What to do? CBT, meditation, spiritual practices - all these are tools for the same thing: **knocking other people's bricks out of your ego**. 

Here is the attitude: “I must earn a lot, otherwise I am a failure.” Whose is it? Yours? Or a dad who grew up poor? Or a society that measures people by salary? 

Here’s another: “You can’t refuse people, it’s selfishness.” Whose? Yours? Or a mother who sacrificed herself all her life? 

Each brick that you realize is foreign - and you consciously remove - makes you **more whole**. A false belief is replaced by an adequate one. Yours. Not imposed. 

“I earn as much as I need for a comfortable life” - instead of a race. 

“I have the right to say no” - instead of eternal sacrifice. 

“I don’t need to be perfect to be worthy of love” - instead of the excellent student syndrome. 

### How a mask creates a spasm 

This is not an abstraction - here is a specific chain that I have seen dozens of times: 

You work in an office. The boss is a toxic tyrant who humiliates you in front of your colleagues. Everything is boiling inside of you. The jaw clenches. Fists clench. You want to stand up and say: *"Don't you dare talk to me in that tone"*. But the mask of a “good employee” does not allow it. You smile, nod and swallow silently. 

Where does this anger go? It doesn't evaporate. It goes into the **trapezius, neck and diaphragm**. The muscles contract, but do not receive a release. After a month of this regime, you have “inexplicable” unsteadiness and a “cotton” head. And six months later - full-fledged PPPD. 

Here are other examples of false bricks that I have seen in people with PPPD: 

* **“I must be an ideal mother”** - woman, 38 years old. She devoted 100% of her time to the children, leaving not a single hour for herself. The feeling of guilt for going to a cafe without children was stronger than the fear of dizziness. When she first said in therapy, “I have the right to 2 hours a week for myself,” her unsteadiness decreased by 30% in a month. Not from pills. From allowing yourself to be imperfect.

* **“A real man doesn’t complain”** - man, 42 years old. I suffered from back pain, insomnia and chronic fatigue for 3 years. He told his wife “everything is fine.” When the doctor told him, “You have PPPD, you need psychotherapy,” he replied, “I’m not crazy.” Left. He returned after 8 months when he stopped leaving the house. The brick “men don’t go to psychologists” almost cost him his career. 

* **“I have to save everyone”** - woman, 51 years old. Nurse. I took night shifts for colleagues. Helped neighbors, relatives, and stray cats. My health came last. PPPD began after she cared for her sick mother-in-law for 3 months without days off. When she was asked in therapy: * “Who is caring for you?” * she began to cry. For the first time in 20 years. 

## The world is objectively sunny, clear and red 

When you start knocking out false bricks, a strange thing happens. The world is becoming... simpler. Easier. Brighter. 

Not because the world has changed. But because you took off a ton of other people’s junk and finally see it for what it is. No alarm filters. Without installations through which everything seemed gray. 

The world is objectively sunny. Clear. Beautiful. 

You just have to take off someone else's glasses. 

## Practice: Inspection of bricks 

### Step 1. Settings table 
Take a piece of paper. Divide it into three columns: 

| Installation | Whose is it? | My version | 
| :--- | :--- | :--- | 
| "I must be strong" | Dad. He grew up without a father and decided that boys don't cry. | “I have the right to be weak. Weakness is not a shame, it is a signal to rest.” | 
| “You’re nothing without money” | Society. Advertising. Instagram. | “My worth is not measured by my salary. I am valuable in myself." | 
| “You can’t let people down” | Mother. She sacrificed herself all her life. | “I let myself down every time I say yes against my will.” | 

### Step 2. Friend/foe test 
For each setting in the table, ask yourself three questions: 
1. **When did I first hear this?** If the answer is “in childhood” or “from my parents,” there is a 90% chance that it is not yours. 
2. **Is this attitude helping me right now?** If it causes anxiety, guilt or shame, it is harmful. 
3. **If I grew up on a desert island, would I come to this belief on my own?** If not, it’s an imposed code. 

### Step 3. Strikethrough Ritual 
Installations that did not pass the test are crossed out in red. Write your version next to it. Adequate. Healthy. Read it out loud three times. Hang it on the refrigerator.
