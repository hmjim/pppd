# The Ego: Dismantling Your False Identity Brick by Brick

*You think you are your name, your job title, your social status, and your set of habits. What if none of that is actually you?*

---

## Advanced Level

If you have made it to this chapter, you already have the fundamentals down. You understand what PPPD is. You are doing the physical work with your body. You have mapped out the adrenaline loop. You are practicing CBT.

Now we go deeper.

There is only the **here and now**. The past no longer exists — it is gone. The future does not exist yet — it hasn't arrived. There is only this exact moment. And right inside this moment, I am going to ask you a few questions capable of blowing the doors off how you see yourself.

Who is experiencing your cognitive dissonance?

Who is feeling your anxiety?

Who is feeling the dizziness?

**Who are you, really?**

## "I" Am Not My Labels

Not "me" as a name on a passport. Not "me" as a software engineer, manager, mom, or accountant. Not "me" as a "good person" or a "bad person." Not "me" as a "family person" or a "loner."

None of those labels are you. All of that is **the ego**.

The ego is a construct — a wall built out of bricks handed to you by other people. Your parents handed you the first stack: *"You have to be obedient,"* *"Boys don't cry,"* *"Good girls don't get angry."* Preschool, school, the military, and workplaces handed you more. The neighborhood, the internet, and advertising supplied the rest.

You never chose those bricks. They were **installed in you**.

## The Coca-Cola Commercial

Maybe this sounds like a silly example, yet it exposes the mechanism cleanly.

Remember those classic holiday Coca-Cola commercials? New Year's snow, glowing red trucks, Santa Claus, *"Holidays are coming."* They played on television every winter since you were a kid. Fast-forward to today, and there sits a can of Coca-Cola right on your holiday dinner table.

Congratulations. That choice wasn't yours. It was planted in your head.

Left to yourself, you didn't even want a Coke. Maybe you wanted hot tea with lemon — or nothing at all. Except sharp executives at ad agencies spent decades wiring a clean association into your brain: *"Holidays = Coca-Cola = happiness."*

Now scale that mechanism up across your entire life.

## Borrowed Paradigms of Happiness

Condo, car, marriage, career ladder. Helicopter, yacht, penthouse — for those who "achieved more." That entire blueprint for living was drafted on our behalf before we ever got a vote. Climb Maslow's pyramid rung by rung, check every box, and happiness is supposed to be waiting at the summit.

Stop and actually examine that. Is it true? Is a car really a unit of human happiness?

Talk honestly with people who already have the car, the house, and the picture-perfect family — plenty of them are more miserable than you are. Why? Because they spent years chasing somebody else's template, finally caught it, and found hollow emptiness inside. Emptiness breeds existential anxiety. And chronic anxiety says: *"Hello, PPPD."*

## What Does Dizziness Have to Do with This?

Everything.

When you spend years living a life that isn't yours — obeying borrowed rules, suffocating your real desires, tolerating what you have no business tolerating — your body starts to scream. Because the body doesn't know how to lie.

You can fool your intellect all day long: *"Everything's fine — I like my job, my marriage, my city."* Your body hears a very different story. So it fires a warning flare — quiet at first (chronic fatigue, tight shoulders), then louder (tension headaches, muscle spasms), until finally it pulls the alarm at full volume (panic attacks, dizziness, PPPD).

**Chronic neurosis is your body screaming when you betray yourself.**

## Knocking Out Borrowed Bricks

So what do we do? CBT, meditation, contemplative inner work — at bottom, these are all chisels built for the exact same job: **knocking other people's bricks out of your ego**.

Take this rule: *"I have to make a lot of money, otherwise I'm a failure."* Whose brick is it? Yours? Or your father's, because he grew up poor? Or a culture that grades human beings by their salary?

Take another: *"Saying no to people is selfish."* Whose voice is that? Yours? Or your mother's, who sacrificed herself for everyone around her?

Every foreign brick you recognize as borrowed — and consciously pry out — makes you **more whole**. In place of a false belief steps a grounded, healthy one that is genuinely yours, not imposed from outside:

*"I earn what I actually need for a comfortable, grounded life"* — instead of an endless rat race.

*"I have every right to say no"* — instead of lifelong martyrdom.

*"I don't need to be flawless to be worthy of love"* — instead of Overachiever Syndrome.

### How a Social Mask Creates Physical Muscle Spasm

This is not abstract philosophy — here is the concrete physiological chain I have watched unfold dozens of times:

You work in an office. Your boss is a toxic tyrant who humiliates you in front of your coworkers. Inside, everything is boiling. Your jaw clenches. Your fists ball up under the table. Every fiber of you wants to stand up and say: *"Do not dare speak to me in that tone."* Except your "good employee" mask won't allow it. So you smile, nod, and swallow your rage in silence.

Where does all that mobilized anger go? It doesn't evaporate. It goes straight into your **upper trapezius, neck muscles, and diaphragm**. Those muscles contract for battle and never get a release. After a month of living like that, you develop "unexplained" unsteadiness and cotton-wool brain fog. Six months later, you have full-blown PPPD.

Here are three more real-world examples of false bricks I have seen in people with PPPD:

* **"I have to be the perfect mother"** — Woman, 38. Gave 100% of her waking hours to her kids, leaving zero time for herself. Her guilt over sitting in a café without her children was stronger than her fear of dizziness. One month after she said out loud in therapy for the first time, *"I have a right to 2 hours a week just for myself,"* her unsteadiness dropped by 30%. Not from medication — from giving herself permission to be imperfect.

* **"Real men don't complain"** — Man, 42. Gritted his teeth through back pain, insomnia, and chronic exhaustion for 3 years. Told his wife, *"Everything's fine."* When a doctor told him, *"You have PPPD — you need psychotherapy,"* he replied, *"I'm not crazy,"* and walked out. Eight months later he came back after becoming completely housebound. That single brick — *"men don't go to therapists"* — nearly cost him his career.

* **"I have to save everyone"** — Woman, 51, nurse. Covered night shifts for coworkers, helped neighbors, bailed out relatives, fed stray cats. Put her own health dead last. Her PPPD began after 3 straight months of caring for her sick mother-in-law without a single day off. When her therapist asked her, *"And who takes care of you?"* — she cried for the first time in 20 years.

## The World Is Objectively Sunny, Clear, and Vibrant

Once you start knocking out false bricks, something remarkable happens. The world starts feeling… simpler. Lighter. Brighter.

Not because the world itself changed — because you finally set down a ton of other people's junk and can see reality for what it actually is. Without anxiety filters. Without the gray, joyless conditioning lenses you used to look through.

Objectively, the world is sunny. Clear. Beautiful.

You just have to take off somebody else's glasses.

## Practice: Auditing Your Bricks

### Step 1. Core Beliefs Table
Take a sheet of paper and divide it into three columns:

| Core Belief ("Brick") | Whose Voice Is It? | My Own Version |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| *"I must always be strong."* | Dad. He grew up without a father and decided boys don't cry. | *"I have a right to feel depleted. Exhaustion isn't shameful — it's a signal to rest."* |
| *"Without big money, you're nobody."* | Society. Advertising. Instagram. | *"My worth isn't measured by a paycheck. I have value simply as I am."* |
| *"You must never let people down."* | Mom. She sacrificed herself her entire life. | *"I let myself down every time I say 'yes' against my own will."* |

### Step 2. The "Mine vs. Implanted" Test
For every belief in your table, ask yourself three diagnostic questions:
1. **When did I first hear this?** If the answer is *"in childhood"* or *"from my parents,"* there is a 90% chance it isn't originally yours.
2. **Is this belief helping me right now?** If it generates chronic anxiety, guilt, or shame, it is harming you.
3. **If I had grown up on a deserted island, would I have arrived at this belief on my own?** If not, it is implanted external code.

### Step 3. The Strikethrough Ritual
Take a red pen and cross out every belief that failed the test. Right next to it, write your own version — grounded, realistic, and healthy. Read each new version out loud three times, then tape the sheet to your fridge.
