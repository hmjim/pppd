# Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) for Dizziness and PPPD 

*How working with thoughts and behavior helps eliminate chronic unsteadiness and fear of falling.*

---

## Why CBT is the gold standard method for PPPD 

According to international clinical protocols, **Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT)** is one of the two main methods of treating PPPD (together with vestibular rehabilitation). 

PPPD is not maintained by physical damage to the ear, but by a **vicious cycle of anxiety**: 
`Dizziness → Anxious thought (“I will fall / seriously ill”) → Splash of adrenaline → Muscle tightening and hypercontrol → Increased dizziness. 

CBT breaks this vicious circle at the thoughts and reactions stage. 

## Main targets of CBT for dizziness 

### 1. Cognitive distortions (catastrophizing) 
Patients with PPPD often interpret any normal body signal as a disaster: 
- *“I swayed, which means I’m about to lose consciousness”* 
- *“The store has become cloudy, which means there will be a stroke”* 

CBT teaches you to identify these thoughts and replace them with realistic, evidence-based alternatives. 

### 2. Avoidance behavior 
When a person feels sick in a store or on the street, the natural reaction is to stop going there. However, avoidance only reinforces the brain's idea that the place is truly dangerous. 

### 3. Protective behavior 
- Walking only along walls 
- Convulsive holding of handrails or a companion 
- Constantly “scanning” your body 

CBT helps you gradually give up “crutches” so that the brain becomes convinced of its own ability to maintain balance. 

## Step-by-step algorithm for working in CBT 

1. **Digitization of the state:** Assessment of the level of anxiety and disability (HADS, DHI tests). 
2. **Exposure therapy:** Step-by-step return to frightening places without the use of protective behavior. 
3. **Recalibrating Interpretations:** Teaching that PPPV unsteadiness is a harmless false signal from the brain and not a threat to life.
