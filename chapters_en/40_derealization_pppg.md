# Derealization in PPPD - why the world seems unreal 

*If, in addition to instability, your world has also become “unreal” - as through glass, as in a dream, as in a movie - this is derealization. Let's figure out why it occurs with PPPD and what to do about it.* 

## What is derealization 

**Derealization** is a feeling of unreality of the surrounding world: 
- Everything is like “behind glass” or “in an aquarium” 
- The world looks flat, “cardboard” 
- Colors appear faded or too bright 
- Sounds are muffled or, on the contrary, harsh 
- Feeling of “fog” or “veil” before the eyes 
- Familiar places seem unfamiliar 

**Depersonalization** - similar, but to yourself: 
- “My hands are not mine” 
- “I watch myself from the outside” 
- “My emotions have dulled” 
- “I’m like a robot” 

## Why derealization occurs in PPPD 

This is **not psychosis**. You're not going crazy. This is a protective reaction of the brain. 

Imagine a fuse in an electrical panel. When the load on the wiring exceeds the permissible limit, the fuse is knocked out so that the wiring does not burn out. The lights go out, but the house is intact. 

With PPPD, anxiety goes off scale so much that the brain turns on a “fuse” - it reduces the sensitivity of perception. The world fades, emotions dull, you feel “behind glass.” It's inconvenient, but it's **overload protection**. 

### Mechanism 

1. **Chronic stress** → constant release of cortisol and adrenaline 
2. **Nervous system overload** → the brain cannot cope with the flow of signals 
3. **Enabling “economy mode”** → reducing the brightness of perception = derealization 
4. **Fear of derealization** → even more anxiety → even more derealization 

This is the same [adrenaline loop](09_adrenaline_loop.html), only manifested through perception, and not through instability. 

## Relationship between derealization and other symptoms of PPPD 

Derealization in PPPD almost always comes with: 
- [Fog in the head](33_brain_fog.html) - heaviness, “cotton wool”, lethargy 
- [Visual dependence](08_visual_dependence.html) - visual overload also provokes derealization 
- [Muscle armor](04_muscle_armor.html) - tight neck → impaired blood flow → increased “fog” 
- Anxiety and panic attacks - derealization often accompanies panic 

## What does NOT help

- ❌ **Google “derealization is schizophrenia”** - no, with PPPD this is precisely neurotic derealization, not psychotic 
- ❌ **Fight the feeling** - the more you try to “return reality”, the stronger the anxiety and derealization 
- ❌ **Avoid stimuli** - sitting at home in a dark room does not heal, but preserves 
- ❌ **Take nootropics** - mexidol, actovegin, cerebrolysin do not have an evidence base for neurotic derealization 

## What helps 

1. **Accept** - derealization is not dangerous. This is a fuse. When the anxiety level decreases, it will go away on its own. 
2. **Reduce anxiety** - [metacognitive therapy](15_metacognition.html), [relaxation](05_relaxation.html), work with [cognitive distortions](16_cognitive_distortions.html) 
3. **Physical activity** - [sport](13_sport.html) reduces cortisol and restores body awareness 
4. **Exposure** - [gradual immersion](12_exposure.html) into frightening situations teaches the brain that there is no danger 
5. **Normalization of sleep** - [sleep and PPPD](26_sleep.html) are closely related; lack of sleep increases derealization
