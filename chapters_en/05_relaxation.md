# Progressive muscle relaxation according to Jacobson: step-by-step technique

*Why it is impossible to relax “just by force of will” and how to return the body to the basic setting of rest through controlled tension.*

---

## 1. The essence and problems: the “relax” trap

When people advise you from all sides “just relax, don’t be nervous,” it only causes quiet rage. If you could relax, you would have done it a long time ago. But your body doesn't listen. 

In a state of PPPD and chronic anxiety, your muscles are tight 24/7. You wake up already tired, with a wooden neck, clenched jaw and raised shoulders. This is called sensory-motor amnesia: the brain is so accustomed to keeping its muscles toned to protect itself from an imagined threat that it has simply **forgot** what it feels like to be completely relaxed. You try to lie down and let go of control, but your body remains taut. You are lying on the bed, and your shoulders and lower back are still levitating a millimeter from the mattress, ready to jump.

## 2. Physiological explanation: how to deceive a pinched wire

The nervous system is built on the principle of a pendulum. It has two modes:
* **Sympathetic** (fight or flight) - tension, mobilization, adrenaline rush.
* **Parasympathetic** (rest and digest) - restoration, relaxation, peace.

In PPPD, the sympathetic system is overheated. She constantly sends signals to the muscles: “Keep your tone, now we will save ourselves!” The muscles are clamped, sending a return signal to the brain: “We are clamped, which means the danger is real!” The cycle is completed.

To break this loop, physiologist Edmund Jacobson discovered a simple law at the beginning of the 20th century: **after maximum tension of a muscle there is always a period of deep automatic relaxation**. This is pure physiology, a reflex. We don't have to force the brain to calm down directly (that's difficult). We'll go in through the back door - through the skeletal muscles. By straining them to the limit for several seconds, we deplete the supply of transmitters in the synapses, and the muscle simply has no choice but to give up and go limp. The brain sees: “The body is relaxed, which means we are safe,” and turns off the alarm siren.

## 3. Cognitive traps: “I’m trying to relax”

The main mistake beginners make is trying to control relaxation. 
“Okay, I have to relax my neck... Why doesn’t it relax? Oh God, I can’t even relax, I’m hopeless!” - this is classic rumination, which only adds adrenaline.

Remember: **relaxation is not an action. This is a lack of action.** 
There is no need to *do* relaxation. Your task is to create high-quality *tension*, and relaxation will happen on its own, according to the laws of physics. You just need to take your hands off the steering wheel and watch the warmth and heaviness return to your limbs.

## 4. Point of Support: Insight

>Your body is not an enemy that swings and spins to torment you. This is a frightened beast that has cowered in a corner from endless mental lashing. Stop hitting him with your thoughts. Give him the physiological command “hang up.”

## 5. Practice: Progressive muscle relaxation (PMR)

Perform this complex every evening before going to bed. It will take exactly 20 minutes.

### Instructions for performing the cycle:
1. **Pose:** Lying on your back, arms extended along the body, eyes closed.
2. **Breathing:** Take a deep breath, hold your breath and while holding it, strongly tense the target muscle group for **7 seconds**. Strain until you tremble, but without pain.
3. **Reset:** Exhale sharply through your mouth (“Haaaah”) and instantly release tension. Imagine that the strings that held the muscle were cut.
4. **Observation:** Just lie still for 20-30 seconds and listen to the sensations in your relaxed zone. Look for a feeling of warmth, tingling or heaviness.

### Step-by-step protocol:

1. **Feet:** Clench your toes into a fist and pull your feet away from you (as if you were gripping your toes in the sand). Tighten up, hold your breath... Exhale - reset.
2. **Calves:** Pull your toes towards you, pressing your heels to the floor. Feel the tension in your shins... Reset.
3. **Hips and Glutes:** Squeeze your glutes and tighten the front of your thighs, straightening your legs like strings... Reset.
4. **Belly:** Tighten your abs as if you are preparing to take a blow or pull your stomach under your ribs... Reset.
5. **Hands:** Clench your fists tightly until your knuckles are white... Reset.
6. **Forearms and Biceps:** Bend your elbows, pressing your fists to your shoulders, contracting your biceps... Reset.
7. **Chest and shoulder blades:** Bring your shoulder blades together at the back, open your chest... Reset.
8. **Shoulders and Neck:** Raise your shoulders high towards your ears, pull your head into your shoulders... Reset.
9. **Face:** Close your eyes, wrinkle your nose, grit your teeth (without fanaticism) and put your whole face into a “grimace”... Reset.

At the end of the exercise, lie down for 2 minutes, feeling how your body “spreads” across the bed. If everything is done correctly, you will feel a pleasant heaviness, as if you are being pressed into the mattress. This is the normal tone of a healthy body.

## Additional technique: Body Scan

Bodyscan is the second line of defense for those who have a particularly dense muscle shell. Unlike PMR, where you actively tense and relax your muscles, bodyscan is a **passive observation** of sensations in the body.

### How to do it
1. Lie on your back. Close your eyes. Take 3 deep breaths.
2. Bring your attention to the **top of your head**. Don't try to relax - just observe: is there tension here? Warm? Tingling? Heaviness?
3. Slowly “move the beam of attention” down: forehead → eyes → jaw → neck → shoulders → arms → chest → stomach → hips → knees → feet.
4. Stay in each zone for 20–30 seconds. If you find tension, don’t fight it. Mentally say: * “I see you. I let you be."*
5. After completing the full scan, lie down for 2 minutes and observe the general sensation of your body.

**Duration:** 15–20 minutes.

**Why it works:** Bodyscan trains interoception - the brain's ability to sense the body without fear. In people with PPPD, interoception is distorted: any sensation in the body (pulsation, tingling, slight tension) is interpreted as a threat. Bodyscan teaches the brain: *"Feelings in the body are just information, not a danger signal"*.

## Express-PMR: 5-minute version for the office

It is not always possible to lie down and do a complete PMR. Here's a shortened version that you can do while sitting at your desk:

1. **Jaw (10 sec):** Clench your teeth tightly for 5 seconds → relax. Notice how the warmth spreads.
2. **Shoulders (10 sec):** Pull your shoulders towards your ears for 5 seconds → drop down.
3. **Fists (10 sec):** Clench your fists for a maximum of 5 seconds → unclench and straighten your fingers.
4. **Feet (10 sec):** Press your toes inside the shoes for 5 seconds → relax.
5. **Breathing (60 sec):** Close your eyes, do 5 square breathing cycles (inhale 4 sec → hold 4 sec → exhale 4 sec → hold 4 sec).

Do a quick PMR every time you notice that you have clenched your jaw or raised your shoulders towards your ears. Especially useful before the lunch break and at the end of the working day.
