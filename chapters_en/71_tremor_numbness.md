# Tremor and Numbness from Anxiety: Why Your Hands Shake and Your Limbs Go Numb

*Your hands are trembling. Or going numb. Or tingling — like you slept on them wrong. Or your whole body vibrates from the inside — invisible on the outside, but you feel it. You're thinking: "MS? Parkinson's? A tumor?" No. It's your nervous system. Let's break it down.*

## Anxiety Tremor

### How it feels

- Fine trembling in the hands — especially when you extend your arm or pick up a cup
- Your chin or lips quiver
- Internal vibration — "shaking from the inside," but if you look at your hands — barely noticeable
- Muscle twitching — eyelid, finger, calf muscle (fasciculations)
- Shaky legs — knees "tremble," especially while standing

### Why this happens

Same mechanism as always: stress → adrenaline → muscles ready for battle.

Adrenaline is the "fight or flight" hormone. It activates muscle fibers so you can run from a tiger. The muscles get the command "contract!" — but there's nowhere to run. You're sitting in a chair reading work emails. And all that contractile energy escapes as **trembling**.

It's like hitting the gas in a car stuck in neutral. The engine roars, the RPMs spike, but the car doesn't move. All that energy turns into vibration.

### Fasciculations — Muscle Twitching

Muscle twitches (eyelid twitching, finger jumping, leg muscle flickering) are a classic feature of anxiety disorders. They're called **Benign Fasciculation Syndrome** (BFS).

With anxiety, this happens to about every other person. This is **not** a sign of ALS (amyotrophic lateral sclerosis), even though Google will cheerfully serve up that diagnosis in the top results.

The difference: with ALS, real muscle weakness comes first (you literally can't hold an object), then fasciculations follow. With anxiety — fasciculations without weakness. You can lift anything. The muscle twitches, but it works just fine.

## Numbness and Tingling

### How it feels

- A limb "fell asleep" — but you weren't sitting on it
- Tingling in fingers, palms, feet
- A "gloves and socks" sensation — the skin surface feels like it's behind a layer of fabric
- Facial numbness — lips, cheek, half the face
- "Pins and needles" all over the body

### Why this happens

Two mechanisms:

**First — hyperventilation.** When you're anxious, you breathe faster and deeper than necessary. CO₂ levels in the blood drop. This causes **respiratory alkalosis** — a shift in your acid-base balance. Calcium in the blood gets bound up, and nerve endings become hyperexcitable. Result: tingling, numbness, "pins and needles."

This is 100% reversible. Slow your breathing — and within 3–5 minutes the tingling will pass.

**Second — muscle compression.** Chronic spasm of the neck and shoulder muscles compresses peripheral nerves. A tight scalene muscle → thoracic outlet syndrome → numbness and tingling in the arms. A tight piriformis muscle → sciatica → numbness in the leg.

This is where [releasing the muscle armor](04_muscle_armor.html) and [relaxation](05_relaxation.html) come in.

### But what if it's multiple sclerosis?

Here's where that fear comes from — Google. You typed "numbness in hands causes" — and the top result: "multiple sclerosis."

Fact: in MS, numbness is **persistent** (days to weeks), usually one-sided, accompanied by real weakness and visual disturbances. In anxiety disorders — it's **migratory** (5 minutes here, then over there), bilateral, resolves on its own, with no weakness.

If you've had an MRI of the brain and spinal cord — and there are no demyelination lesions — you don't have MS. Period. Stop Googling.

## What to Do

1. **Stop Googling symptoms.** Every search for "numbness in hands causes" ends with ALS, MS, or a brain tumor. Google is not a doctor. Google is a [health anxiety](11_hypochondria.html) generator.

2. **Breathe properly.** If you have tingling — you're probably hyperventilating. Slow your breathing: inhale 4 seconds, exhale 6–8 seconds. The tingling will be gone in 3 minutes.

3. **Release the muscle spasm.** [Jacobson's Progressive Relaxation](05_relaxation.html) — daily. Neck and shoulder stretches. This will relieve the nerve compression.

4. **Move your body.** [Exercise](13_sport.html) "burns off" adrenaline — the very energy that's escaping as trembling. After a good workout, the tremor disappears for hours. With regular exercise — it goes away for good.

5. **Work on the anxiety itself.** [CBT](34_cbt_for_dizziness.html), [metacognitive therapy](15_metacognition.html). Lower your baseline anxiety → lower your adrenaline levels → tremor and numbness fade away.
