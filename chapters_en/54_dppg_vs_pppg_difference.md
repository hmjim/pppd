# BPPV and PPPD: Why You're Still Dizzy After the Crystals Were Fixed

*Your doctor treated your BPPV with an Epley maneuver. They said — "all done, the crystals are back in place." But a week went by, a month, three — and you're still unsteady. The spinning attack ended, but your body never forgot. Sound familiar? Welcome to PPPD.*

## BPPV and PPPD Are Different Conditions

Let's lay this out clearly, because the confusion here is rampant.

| | BPPV | PPPD |
|---|---|---|
| **What it is** | Calcium crystals (otoconia) that drifted into the semicircular canal of the inner ear | The brain is "stuck" in a mode of anxious hypervigilance |
| **What you feel** | Violent spinning — the room rotates | Unsteadiness, "rocking," brain fog, jelly legs |
| **When** | 10–60 seconds when turning your head or rolling over in bed | Constantly: for hours, days, months |
| **Nystagmus** | Present — clear, visible on Dix-Hallpike test | Absent — all tests come back normal |
| **Treatment** | Epley / Sémont / Barbecue maneuver — 1–3 sessions | [Vestibular exercises](06_vestibular.html) + [CBT](34_cbt_for_dizziness.html) + [exposure therapy](12_exposure.html) — 2–6 months |

BPPV is a **hardware** problem. A mechanical issue: a crystal is in the wrong place. Fix it — it works.

PPPD is a **software** problem. A programming error: the brain is stuck in a loop. The crystal has been back in place for ages, but the brain still "remembers" the attack.

## Why PPPD Starts After BPPV

Here's how it happens. Step by step.

**Step 1.** You had a BPPV attack. You rolled over in bed — and the world exploded. The room is spinning, you're nauseous, gripping the mattress, heart pounding at 150. You genuinely think you're dying. Ambulance, emergency room, sheer terror.

**Step 2.** The doctor performed the Epley maneuver. The otoconia went back into place. The spinning stopped. The physical problem is solved.

**Step 3.** But your brain sustained a **massive shock**. That attack was so terrifying that the amygdala (the brain's fear center) logged it as a "mortal threat." And now it stands guard.

**Step 4.** You start being afraid to turn your head. You lock your neck. You walk like a robot — cautiously, slowly, controlling every movement. This is protective behavior.

**Step 5.** The brain switches to [visual control](08_visual_dependence.html) of balance. It no longer trusts the vestibular system — "that thing already let me down once." Visual dependence → discomfort in stores, on public transit, from screens.

**Step 6.** Constant anxious scanning — "is it spinning again?" — feeds the [adrenaline loop](09_adrenaline_loop.html). Adrenaline → neck muscle spasm → distorted proprioception → feeling of unsteadiness → even more anxiety.

**Step 7.** Weeks go by, months. BPPV was cured long ago. But the unsteadiness remains. This is PPPD.

According to research, up to **30% of PPPD cases** develop after BPPV. It's one of the most common triggers.

## "But Maybe the Crystals Shifted Again?"

The classic question. And the classic trap.

If the Epley maneuver was performed correctly and confirmed by a negative Dix-Hallpike test — the crystals are in place. Period. BPPV recurrence is possible, but it presents as **rotational vertigo when turning the head**, not constant unsteadiness.

If you're **constantly** wobbly, swaying, feeling off — without violent spinning — that's not BPPV. That's PPPD. And treating it with the Epley maneuver is like trying to fix a virus-infected computer by rebooting it. Sure, it rebooted — but the virus is still there.

## What to Do

Stop going to the ENT asking them to "check the crystals one more time." If the neuro-otologist confirmed that BPPV is cured — trust them.
