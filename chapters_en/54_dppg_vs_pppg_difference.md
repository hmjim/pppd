# BPPV vs. PPPD: Differences and Why BPPV Often Triggers Chronic PPPD

In clinical practice, up to 30% of all PPPD cases develop immediately following an acute episode of **BPPV (Benign Paroxysmal Positional Vertigo)**.

Patients frequently confuse these two distinct conditions, spending years hunting for phantom “inner ear crystals” that have long since dissolved.

---

## 1. Diagnostic Comparison: BPPV vs. PPPD

| Parameter | BPPV (Canalithiasis) | PPPD (Functional Sensory Mismatch) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Pathology** | Physical displacement of calcium carbonate otolith crystals into semicircular canals. | Central sensory processing error + chronic autonomic hypervigilance. |
| **Sensation** | Violent, rotational room-spinning vertigo (“carousel illusion”). | Swaying, unsteadiness, floating, heavy head, “spongy floor” walking. |
| **Duration** | 10–60 seconds strictly provoked by head position change in bed. | Continuous, persisting for hours, days, and months on end. |
| **Nystagmus** | Characteristic torsional/horizontal nystagmus during Dix-Hallpike maneuver. | Completely absent. Organic neuro-otologic tests are normal. |
| **Treatment** | Repositioning maneuvers (Epley, Semont, BBQ Roll) in 1–3 sessions. | Vestibular rehabilitation (VRT), CBT, SSRIs, nervous system recalibration. |

---

## 2. Why Does Chronic PPPD Start After Otolith Repositioning?

The Epley maneuver restores displaced otoliths in a matter of minutes. However, a violent initial vertigo attack triggers profound autonomic panic, nausea, and terror.

The brain perceives this vestibular trauma as a life-threatening crisis and locks into **emergency defense mode**:
* The patient freezes their neck;
* Avoids looking up or turning their head;
* Continuously scans their balance: *“Is the room going to spin again?”*

This hyper-protective somatic state becomes hardwired into cerebellar pathways, mutating an acute mechanical incident into chronic PPPD.
