# Recovery stories: living examples of victory over PPPD 

*How real people with severe, persistent dizziness regained their resilience by changing their habits and thinking.*

---

## Case 1. Anton (32 years old, Senior Backend Developer) 
* **Symptoms:** Unsteadiness, wobbly head, inability to look at the monitor (visual addiction), panic when scrolling code, muscle block in the neck. Duration of illness: 1.5 years. 
* **Trigger:** Deadline + processing + coffee 6 cups a day for 3 months. 
* **Trap:** I tried to cure PPPD through “Doctor shopping” - I went through 4 MRIs, an ultrasound scan, donated blood for all markers, took a handful of nootropics and vascular drugs. The result is zero. I spent more than 200,000 rubles on “treatment.” 
* **How it came out:** 
1. **Digitization (Chapter 3):** Passed HADS (alarm 16/21) and DHI (68/100). I saw the numbers and stopped considering myself dying. 
2. **Closed the door to the clinic.** Recognized that it was a software failure, not a hardware failure. 
3. **Body (Chapters 4-5):** Introduced daily relaxation according to Jacobson - relieved neck spasm in 2 weeks. VOR exercises twice a day. 
4. **Key Point:** The first 5 days of VOR training the unsteadiness **increased**. Anton read Chapter 14 (neuroplasticity), realized that the brain is being rewired, and continued. 
5. **Thinking (Chapter 16):** Started a SMER diary. Discovered that his main distortion is Catastrophization. In a month I caught 47 catastrophic thoughts and rewrote them. 
* **Result:** After 2 months I fully returned to working behind the monitor. HADS-A: 16 → 6. DHI: 68 → 18. Unsteadiness is gone by 95%, rare rebounds when overtired are perceived as a “time to sleep” sensor.

## Case 2. Elena (45 years old, chief accountant) 
* **Symptoms:** Feeling of rocking “like on the deck of a ship” 24/7, panic attacks in supermarkets, fear of queues, complete refusal to travel on public transport. Duration of illness: 3 years. 
* **Trigger:** Death of mother + annual report + conflict with husband - three blows in 2 months. Holmes-Rahe scale: 420 points. 
* **Trap:** Total control (excellent student syndrome). All my life I was burdened with family and work, unable to relax. I've been accumulating stress for years. Illness has become a “legal” way to stop. 
* **How it came out:** 
1. **Audit of areas of life (Chapter 17):** Conducted an honest written audit. I discovered a secondary benefit: illness was the only legal reason to tell my husband and children “I’m tired, do it yourself” and not take on additional work. 
2. **Ego and bricks (Chapter 18):** The brick “a good wife does everything herself” is mom’s. Crossed it out. She wrote: “A good wife is one who is alive and well.” 
3. **Boundaries and Exposure (Chapter 12):** Established a strict “no” rule for households and management. I built a staircase for the exhibition: from standing on the porch to traveling on the bus. I applied the “Horizon Rule” outside. 
* **Result:** Removed hyper-responsibility. After 3 months, she completely restored her vegetation, goes to shopping centers without fear, and rides the subway. DHI: 82 → 22.

## Case 3. Maxim (28 years old, marketer, traveler) 
* **Symptoms:** Constant dizziness after suffering from otitis, derealization (the world is like behind glass), severe fear of fainting, hypochondria. Duration of illness: 11 months. 
* **Trigger:** Acute vestibular neuritis → poor-quality rehabilitation → anxious fixation. 
* **Pitfall:** CAS syndrome (persistent internal scanner) and Google addiction. I woke up and checked “how is my head?”, read neurotic forums for 3 hours a day. Over the course of 11 months, I Googled for myself: brain tumor, multiple sclerosis, Parkinson’s disease and aneurysm. Of course, he had none of these diagnoses. 
* **How it came out:** 
1. **Quarantine (Chapter 11):** Implemented the “Cold Turkey” protocol - blocked all medical sites, deleted chats of neurotics, put on the screensaver: “I’m healthy. I’m not allowed to Google.” 
2. **Grounding (Chapter 10):** Every time I turned on the scanner, I did the “5-4-3-2-1” practice. 
3. **Exposition and surrender (Chapter 12):** When panicking on the street, I used Frankl’s method of paradoxical intention: *“Come on, spin me around even more, throw me on the asphalt!”*. 
* **Result:** The Amygdala quickly calmed down when it lost its supply of fear. After 6 weeks, derealization went away, after 2 months Maxim went on his first vacation by plane. HADS-A: 18 → 5.

## Case 4. Olga (55 years old, mathematics teacher) 
* **Symptoms:** Chronic unsteadiness, fear of leaving the house unaccompanied, insomnia, constant feeling “I’m going to feel bad.” Duration of illness: 4.5 years. 
* **Trigger:** Menopause + retirement + “empty nest” syndrome (children left to study). 
* **Trap:** Olga was the hardest case of all. 4.5 years of illness, total avoidance, complete social isolation. She believed that “at my age it’s too late to change anything.” Brick: “I’m 55, my time has passed.” 
* **How it came out:** 
1. **Basic settings (Chapter 7):** Started with the simplest - 10,000 steps. The first week I only walked around the entrance. The second - to the neighboring house. The third one is to the park. Every day 100 meters more. 
2. **Inner Child (Chapter 19):** Found a photo of myself as a child. I burst into tears. For the first time in 4 years I told myself: “You can be afraid. I'm close." 
3. **Meditation:** Started with 3 minutes a day. In a month - 10 minutes. After 2 months - 20 minutes. Sleep returned to normal for the first time in 3 years. 
* **Result:** After 4 months, Olga rides a minibus alone, goes to the store and signed up for painting courses. Her words: “I lost 4.5 years of my life. But I will live the remaining 30 years differently.”

## Case 5. Dmitry (24 years old, freelance designer) 
* **Symptoms:** Derealization (“the world is in a fog”), panic attacks 3-4 times a week, fear of going crazy, complete refusal of social life. Duration of illness: 8 months. 
* **Trigger:** Protracted COVID-19 + remote isolation + sitting at home for 16 hours every day. 
* **Trap:** Dmitry was sure that he was “brain damaged after Covid” and “nothing will come back.” I read scientific articles about long-COVID, found confirmation of my fears and convinced myself that his case was uniquely severe. 
* **How it came out:** 
1. **Digitization (Chapter 3):** HADS-A: 19/21. DHI: 74/100. Seeing the numbers, I realized for the first time the scale of the problem—and that it was measurable. 
2. **Sports (Chapter 13):** Started with simple squats at home. A week later - walking. A month later - run 2 km. Adrenaline, which had been accumulating in tight muscles for years, began to be utilized. 
3. **Metacognition (Chapter 15):** Mastered the “worry timer” - allocated 15 minutes a day to anxious thoughts, the rest of the time mentally said: “This is not now. There is a special time for this." 
4. **Affirmations (Chapter 13):** Every morning - 5 minutes of affirmations. At first I felt like an idiot. After 3 weeks, I noticed that my morning anxiety decreased by 40%. 
* **Result:** After 3 months, derealization was completely gone. Panic attacks - from 4 times a week to 0. HADS-A: 19 → 7. Got a job in the office - for the first time in a year I went out to work full-time.

## Case 6. Natalia (33 years old, on maternity leave) 
* **Symptoms:** Unsteadiness, “cast iron” head, fear of being alone with the child, fear of dropping the baby due to dizziness. Duration of illness: 14 months. 
* **Trigger:** Difficult childbirth + postpartum depression + chronic lack of sleep + mother-in-law pressure. 
* **Trap:** “I’m a bad mother if I need help.” Natalya refused any help, believing that she had to cope on her own. At the same time, her inner voice constantly said: “What if I drop the child? What if I lose consciousness while I’m feeding?” Every feeding turned into anxiety torture. 
* **How it came out:** 
1. **Ego (Chapter 18):** Dismantled the “good mother = single mother” brick. This was her mother's attitude. For the first time, Natalya asked her husband to take the child for 2 hours to go for a walk alone. 
2. **Neurophysiology (Chapter 7):** I improved my sleep - I agreed with my husband about “on duty.” Started sleeping 6-7 hours instead of 4. Morning restlessness decreased by 50% in the first week. 
3. **Inner Child (Chapter 19):** Wrote to myself: “You have the right to be tired. A tired mother is not a bad mother. A bad mother is a mother who burned out.” 
* **Result:** After 2.5 months, the unsteadiness went away. Natalya walks with a stroller without fear, leaves her child with her husband without guilt, and signed up for postpartum yoga. Her words: “I became a better mother when I stopped being perfect.”

## What unites all these stories? 

Look at these 6 cases. Different people, different ages, different triggers. But there is only one pattern: 

1. **Digitization** - Each of them started with measurement, not panic. 
2. **The body is primary** - everyone started with physical practices (walking, Jacobson, VOR), and not with “reprogramming thinking.” 
3. **Thinking secondary** - cognitive techniques (SERM, metacognition, affirmations) were activated when the body stabilized. 
4. **Root cause** - everyone had a deep-seated false attitude that fueled the neurosis. Removing this setup was more important than removing the shakyness. 
5. **Nonlinearity** - each of them experienced setbacks. Not a single one was cured in a straight line “up”.
